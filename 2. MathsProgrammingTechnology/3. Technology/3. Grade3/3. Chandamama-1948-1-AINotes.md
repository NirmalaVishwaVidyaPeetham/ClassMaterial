### 📝 కథ సారాంశం (English Story Summary)
This musical poem welcomes the joyous festival of Sankranti into Telugu villages. The poet portrays Sankranti as an auspicious maiden arriving gracefully during early dawn. Winter mornings are brisk, misty, and chilly, but villages burst with color and warmth. Golden paddy fields bow down laden with ripe grains, ready for the farmers' sharp sickles.

Every courtyard comes alive with white rangoli designs (*muggulu*) adorned with yellow marigold flowers and cow-dung balls (*gobbemma*). Decorated bulls (*gangireddu*) dance delightfully to drumbeats, while Haridasu singers sing sacred verses at daybreak. Families make delicious festive sweets like *ariselu* and sweet pongal. Because India recently won independence in 1947, the poet enthusiastically sings that this Sankranti brings freedom and sweet hope to all hardworking farmers, artists, and children!

### 🌟 నీతి / ముఖ్య సందేశం (Moral / Key Takeaway)
Sankranti honors Mother Nature, hardworking farmers, and the beauty of sharing harvest blessings together in harmony.

### 💡 ఆలోచించండి (Questions to Think & Discuss)
1. What special sounds and sights wake you up during the festival of Sankranti?
2. Why is harvesting crops so important for our food and village celebrations?

### 📝 కథ సారాంశం (English Story Summary)
Little Chitti wakes up very early during the Sankranti season. She washes her face, sweeps her front yard, sprinkles clean water, and molds holy *gobbemmas* out of cow dung. Decorating them with bright blossoms, she dances happily around them, singing a sweet folk prayer to Gobbidevi.

In her song, she asks goddess Gobbemma to bless her with a sister gentle like a chrysanthemum, a brother radiant like a lotus, and a husband handsome like a fragrant *mogali* flower! Her playful elder cousin brother (*baava*) hides behind the door and hears her wish. He pops out laughing and teases, "Chitti! A *mogali* flower has sharp prickles; won't such a husband prick you?" Blushing with shyness, Chitti giggles and runs straight to hide behind her mother!

### 🌟 నీతి / ముఖ్య సందేశం (Moral / Key Takeaway)
Innocent childhood traditions and family teasing make festive celebrations delightful and memorable.

### 💡 ఆలోచించండి (Questions to Think & Discuss)
- Why do children make sweet wishes to God while playing traditional games?

### 📝 కథ సారాంశం (English Story Summary)
This touching folk poem tells the story of Kamakshi, a gentle young woman married into a very rich family. Despite their wealth, she has to perform tiresome household chores from dawn to dusk. While washing lentils with heavy pots of water, her eldest brother suddenly arrives to invite her home for the Sankranti festival. Seeing him, tears roll down her cheeks because she misses her parents deeply.

Her loving brother tells her, "Wipe your tears, sister, dress up, and ask your in-laws' permission to come home." Kamakshi patiently approaches her mother-in-law sitting on a stool, who directs her to her father-in-law resting on his bed. The father-in-law sends her to her brother-in-law studying the scriptures, who in turn tells her to ask her sister-in-law. Finally, her husband lovingly gives his full permission, asking her to wear her finest silks and golden jewels, and happily visit her parents' home.

### 🌟 నీతి / ముఖ్య సందేశం (Moral / Key Takeaway)
Showing patience, polite respect to family elders, and maintaining loving ties with one's parents brings true happiness.

- How does it feel when loving family members come from far away to take you home for holidays?

### 📝 కథ సారాంశం (English Story Summary)

Shiva commanded his gatekeeper, Nandi, to let no one inside. However, an attendant named Pushpadanta became too curious. Turning himself into a tiny bumblebee, he slipped into the bedchamber and overheard every fascinating tale Shiva narrated. Pushpadanta then went home and whispered these secret stories to his wife, Jaya.

The next morning, Jaya repeated the stories to Parvati! Furious that her exclusive stories were leaked, Parvati cursed Pushpadanta and his friend Malyavanta to be born as mortals on Earth. To be free from the curse, Pushpadanta was to narrate these tales to a forest spirit named Kanabhuti, who in turn would pass them to Malyavanta (born on Earth as the scholar Gunadhya). ### 📝 కథ సారాంశం (English Story Summary)
Gajakarna and Gokarna were two master thieves living in neighboring towns, each convinced that he was the smartest rogue alive. When they met, they secretly plotted to outsmart one another. That night, Gokarna stole Gajakarna’s golden dinner plate by diving into a pond where it was hidden. Seeing how clever Gokarna was, Gajakarna chuckled and suggested: "Brother, instead of stealing from each other, let's become equal partners and steal together!"

They targeted a wealthy merchant's family whose patriarch, Subbanna Setti, had recently passed away. Gokarna dug a secret breathing trench inside the graveyard, and Gajakarna hid underground. Gokarna then brought Setti’s grieving family to the grave and shouted, "Oh spirit of Setti, did you owe me one thousand silver rupees?" From beneath the earth, Gajakarna moaned spookily, "Yes! Give him the thousand rupees!" Convinced it was their ancestor speaking, the terrified family handed over the silver coins! But as soon as Gokarna got the treasure, he tried to run away with all of it. Gajakarna tracked him down, tricked him using single shoes dropped along the road, and retrieved the loot. Realizing they were completely equal in cunning tricks, the two rogues finally split the coins fifty-fifty and vowed never to cheat a partner again!

### 🌟 నీతి / ముఖ్య సందేశం (Moral / Key Takeaway)
Dishonest thieves can never truly trust each other, as trickery always meets matching trickery.

### 📝 కథ సారాంశం (English Story Summary)
A bright, clever student studied under a pious village teacher (*Guru*). When the Guru’s wife gave birth to a baby boy, Lord Brahma, the creator, secretly arrived at night to write the infant’s destiny. The bold student intercepted Brahma at the door and demanded to know what was written. Annoyed, Brahma revealed: "This boy will be bound by lifelong poverty. He will own only a single cow and a small measure of grain (*moodu putlu*); no matter how hard he works, he will never have more!" Years later, when the Guru had a daughter, Brahma wrote: "Every marriage proposal for this girl will be broken off."

When the Guru passed away, his son struggled in poverty, just as Brahma had written. The clever student visited his teacher’s grown son and advised: "Listen to me. Take your grain and feed all the hungry villagers in a free charity feast (*santarpana*)! Sell your cow and give the money away." The son did so. To maintain the law of fate that the boy must always possess his fixed grain and cow, Brahma had to continuously supply fresh sacks of grain and new cattle every single morning! Similarly, the student advised the sister to decline all greedy suitors until only a truly worthy, noble husband arrived. Meeting Brahma later on a misty road, the student smiled knowingly. Brahma laughed, admitting, "Your smart generosity forced me to turn their poverty into boundless abundance!"

### 🌟 నీతి / ముఖ్య సందేశం (Moral / Key Takeaway)
Fearless charity, clever thinking, and generous deeds can overcome even the harshest circumstances.

### 💡 ఆలోచించండి (Questions to Think & Discuss)
- How did sharing food with poor people end up helping the family become rich?
