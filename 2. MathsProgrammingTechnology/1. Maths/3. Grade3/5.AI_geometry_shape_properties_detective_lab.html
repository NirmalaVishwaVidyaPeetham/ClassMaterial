How to Think Like a Geometry Detective: “Must-Have Clues” vs. “Guaranteed Proof Clues”

In grown-up math books, these are called Necessary Conditions and Sufficient Conditions. Here is what they really mean!

1. Necessary (“Must-Have Clue”)

If it's missing, the suspect is innocent!

A Necessary property is something a shape must have. For example:
• “Every Rhombus MUST have perpendicular ($90^\circ$) diagonals.”
• “Every Dog MUST have 4 legs.”

⚠️ The Detective Trap: Just because an animal has 4 legs doesn't prove it's a dog (it could be a cat!). And just because a shape has $90^\circ$ diagonals doesn't prove it's a Rhombus—a Kite has $90^\circ$ diagonals too!
2. Sufficient (“Case-Closed Guarantee!”)

No impostor can fake this clue combo!

A Sufficient condition is a clue (or team of clues) so specific that only your target shape (or its special upgraded version) can pass!

✓ To Guarantee a Parallelogram: Diagonals bisect each other.
✓ To Guarantee a Rhombus: Diagonals bisect each other AND cross at $90^\circ$ (kicks out the Kite!).
✓ To Guarantee a Rectangle: Diagonals bisect each other AND have equal length (kicks out the Trapezoid!).
✓ To Guarantee a Square: Diagonals bisect each other AND cross at $90^\circ$ AND have equal length!
