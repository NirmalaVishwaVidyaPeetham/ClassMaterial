## Chapter 1: Migration of People

### 1. What is Migration?

* **Definition:** **Migration** is the seasonal or permanent movement of people from one place to another in search of a better livelihood.
* **Impact on Families & Children:**
  * Migration changes the structure of families (breaking joint families into smaller units).
  * Children are either taken along to the new place (which disrupts their regular schooling) or left behind in the village with grandparents or guardians.

### 2. Reasons for Migration

People migrate for two main categories of reasons:

| **2. Economic & Social Reasons** | **Poverty**, unemployment, daily wage labour, **job transfers**, starting a business, or **higher education**. | **Kumari’s father** moved to Chennai as a daily wage labourer (earns more, but the cost of living is higher). **Praveen’s father** (a bank employee) got transferred from Madanapalle to Tirupati. **Pallavi’s family** moved to Guntur for her brother's college education and her father's new business. |

* **Nomads (*Sanchara Jathulu*):** People who do not live in a permanent house and continuously move from place to place in search of livelihood (e.g., *Somaiah*, whose education is affected by constant movement).
* **Seasonal Migrant Labourers:** In **Prakasam district**, **tobacco** is the main crop. During the harvest season, **tobacco pluckers (*Pachhaku* labourers)** migrate with their families from neighbouring districts to Prakasam; their children temporarily attend the nearest Government schools.

### 3. Effects of Migration

1. **Child Labour:** When a migrant father’s earnings in a city are insufficient due to high living costs, children are sometimes forced to work instead of attending school (e.g., Rajaiah’s son *Srinu* in Chennai).
2. **Improved Economic Status:** Skilled migration abroad or to cities can improve a family's standard of living (e.g., *Madhu* worked abroad, educated his children, built a house, bought a farm, and retired peacefully in his village).
3. **Loss of Rural Community Life:** Migrant labourers in big cities (like *Babulu’s family* who moved from Chalama village to Visakhapatnam) often miss the mutual support, farming, and community bond of village life.
4. **Formation of Urban Slums:** Rapid migration from rural villages to cities causes overcrowding, temporary shelters, scarcity of clean water/sanitation, poverty, and an informal economy—leading to the growth of **slums**. To reduce migration, the government implements **rural development programmes** to create jobs in villages.

## Chapter 2: Climate Change

### 1. Climate, Climate Change, and Global Warming

* **Climate:** The long-term weather pattern of an area, determined by **temperature, rainfall, humidity, and air pressure**.
* **Climate Change:** Abnormal variations in seasonal weather patterns—such as unseasonal rains during harvest, delayed monsoons, and prolonged, scorching summers—caused by human interference with nature.
* **Global Warming:** The gradual, continuous increase in the Earth’s average atmospheric and ocean temperatures due to heat-trapping gases.

### 2. Causes of Climate Change and Global Warming

1. **Deforestation:** Indiscriminately cutting down forests and converting grasslands into farmlands, roads, factories, buildings, and dams.
2. **Burning Fossil Fuels & Waste:** Burning coal, petrol, diesel, wood, dry leaves, and garbage.
3. **Industrial & Vehicular Emissions:** Factories and motor vehicles release toxic gases into the air and chemical effluents into water bodies.
4. **Cooling Appliances:** Indiscriminate use of **air conditioners (ACs) and refrigerators**, which emit harmful greenhouse chemicals into the atmosphere.
5. **Plastic Pollution:** Dumping non-biodegradable single-use plastics (use-and-throw pens, carry bags, cups, plates) that choke soil and water bodies.

> **Climate Inequality Note:** Wealthy individuals and **developed countries** contribute the most to global warming (through heavy industry, cars, and ACs), but poor roadside workers, farmers, and **developing countries** suffer the worst consequences of unseasonal rains and droughts.

### 3. Adverse Effects of Climate Change

1. **Droughts and Floods:** Failure of timely monsoon rains causes severe **droughts**, while sudden cloudbursts and heavy cyclones cause destructive **floods and tsunamis**.
2. **Melting of Polar Ice Caps:** Rising global temperatures melt polar ice, causing **sea levels to rise** and submerging coastal/seashore towns.
3. **Death of Marine Life:** Warming ocean waters destroy coral reefs, aquatic plants, and fish.
4. **Forest Fires (Bushfires):** Extreme atmospheric heat and human negligence trigger massive forest fires.
   * *Case Study – Australian Bushfires (January $2020$):* Declared a global environmental emergency; scorched millions of acres of forest and **killed over $500\text{ million}$ wild animals**.

### 4. Dangers of Plastics and Newspaper Food Packing

* **Hot Food in Plastic:** Carrying hot tea, coffee, or curries in thin plastic bags or drinking from substandard plastic cups causes toxic chemicals from the plastic to dissolve into the food.
* **Newspaper Packing Hazard:** Street vendors and hotels often wrap hot fried food in newspapers. This is dangerous because **newspaper printing inks** contain harmful chemicals that contaminate the food.
* **Soil & Animal Harm:** Plastics are **non-biodegradable** (cannot decompose in soil). They block water percolation into the soil, clog canals, and kill cows and buffaloes that swallow plastic food wrappers.

### 5. Saving Our Forests ("Lungs of the Earth")

* **Why Forests are Called the "Lungs of the Earth":** Trees absorb carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) and release life-giving **oxygen ($\text{O}_2$)**. They bring rain, prevent soil erosion, maintain ecological balance, and provide food, fuel, timber, medicine, and shelter for wildlife.
* **Environmental Champions:**
  * **The Chipko Movement ($1970\text{s}$):** An organized grassroots forest conservation movement in India. The word ***'Chipko'*** means **'to embrace' or 'to hug'**. Villagers (especially women) hugged forest trees to stop timber contractors from felling them.
  * **Greta Thunberg:** A young **Swedish environmental activist** who gained worldwide recognition after addressing world leaders at the **$2018\text{ United Nations Climate Change Conference}$** to demand urgent action against global warming.

## Chapter 3: Clothes We Wear

### 1. Classification of Fibres and Fabrics

Clothes are woven from threads (yarn) spun from **fibres**. Fibres are classified into two main types:

#### A. Natural Fibres (Obtained from Plants and Animals)

| Fibre Name                          | Source                                                  | Production Process ($4\text{ Steps}$)                                                                                                             | Key Properties & Uses                                                                                                                                                                                  |
|:----------------------------------- |:------------------------------------------------------- |:------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |:------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **1. Cotton** *(Plant Fibre)*       | White fluffy bolls (**fruits**) of the cotton plant.    | **Cotton Fruit $\rightarrow$ Spinning** (into yarn on a wheel) **$\rightarrow$ Weaving** (on a loom) **$\rightarrow$ Cotton Fabric**              | Soft, absorbs sweat easily, allows air circulation, and keeps the body cool in **summer / hot climates**.                                                                                              |
| **2. Jute & Flax** *(Plant Fibres)* | Stem of Jute and Flax plants.                           | Fibres extracted from stems and woven.                                                                                                            | **Jute** is used to make gunny bags, ropes, carpets, and designer wear. **Flax** is used to make **Linen** cloth.                                                                                      |
| **3. Silk** *(Animal Fibre)*        | **Silkworm** (caterpillar of the silk moth).            | **Silkworm** (feeds on **mulberry leaves**) **$\rightarrow$ Cocoon $\rightarrow$ Boiling & Spinning $\rightarrow$ Silk Saree/Fabric**             | Smooth, lustrous, and expensive. *Note:* Cocoons are **boiled in hot water** to kill the caterpillar inside; otherwise, the silk moth breaks the cocoon to emerge and cuts the continuous silk thread. |
| **4. Wool** *(Animal Fibre)*        | Thick hair/fur (**fleece**) of **Sheep** (or yak/goat). | **Sheep $\rightarrow$ Shearing** (cutting fleece) **$\rightarrow$ Reeling** (spinning into wool yarn) **$\rightarrow$ Knitting/Weaving Sweaters** | Traps body heat and keeps us warm in **winter / cold regions**.                                                                                                                                        |

#### B. Artificial / Synthetic Fibres (Man-Made)

* Manufactured in factories using **chemical processes** (petroleum/coal derivatives).
* **Examples:** **Polyester, Terylene, Rayon, and Nylon**.
* **Properties:** Strong, wrinkle-free, affordable, durable (last longer), and dry very quickly.
* **Waterproof Fabrics:** Umbrellas and raincoats worn during the rainy season are made of synthetic waterproof materials that do not let water pass through.

### 2. Need for Clothes, Traditional Dresses, and Care

* **Why We Wear Clothes:**
  1. Protect our body from scorching heat, freezing cold, and rain.
  2. Absorb sweat and keep the skin cool and clean.
  3. Protect against germs, dust, pollution, and insect bites.
  4. Reflect our regional **culture** and professional identity (**uniforms** worn by students, doctors, nurses, police officers, lawyers, chefs, and astronauts).
* **Traditional Indian Attire by State:**
  * **Andhra Pradesh:** Men wear *dhoti/pancha* and shirt; women wear *saree-blouse*, *Punjabi dress*, or *lehenga-voni*.
  * **Kerala:** Men wear *lungi (mundu)*.
  * **Rajasthan:** Men wear *dhoti*, *angarkha*, and colourful turbans.
  * **West Bengal:** Men wear *kurta-dhoti*; women drape sarees in the traditional Bengali style.
* **Washing and Drying Clothes Properly:**
  * Unwashed clothes soaked in sweat breed bacteria and cause **skin diseases and ringworm**.
  * Clothes should be washed regularly with **detergent soap** (by hand or in a washing machine).
  * **Drying Rule:** White clothes are dried in direct sunlight to kill germs, whereas **dark and brightly coloured clothes must be dried in the shade** so that the sun does not fade their colour.

### 3. Air Around Us and Its Properties

* **Wind:** Moving air is called **wind**. Wind helps wet clothes dry faster (even on cloudy days) by speeding up evaporation.

* **Types of Wind by Speed:**
  
  1. **Breeze:** A gentle, pleasant light wind.
  2. **Gale:** A strong, forceful wind.
  3. **Cyclone:** Violent, high-speed wind accompanied by heavy rain that uproots big trees and blows away house roofs.

* **Wind Power:** **Windmills** use the force of wind to generate electricity (**wind power**) and pump underground water from wells for farming.

* **6 Fundamental Properties of Air:**
  
  1. **Air is needed for breathing** by all living organisms.
  2. **Air occupies space** (seen when inflating a balloon, bicycle tube, football, or swimming tube).
  3. **Air has weight.**
  4. **Air exerts pressure:** Air pressure enables us to **drink juice with a straw, fly a kite, ride a bicycle, and get water through taps from an overhead tank**.
  5. **Air produces and carries sound** (e.g., playing music on a **flute**).
  6. **Air carries smell** (e.g., smelling the fragrance of flowers or food from a distance).

## Chapter 4: Know Our Organ System

### 1. External Organs, Internal Organs, and Organ Systems

* **External Organs:** Body parts visible on the outside (e.g., eyes, ears, nose, hands, legs).
* **Internal Organs:** Delicate organs located inside the body that cannot be seen directly (e.g., brain, heart, lungs, stomach, liver, pancreas, kidneys).
* **Organ System:** A group of organs that work together like parts of a machine to perform a major life function.

### 2. The 6 Major Human Organ Systems

#### 1. The Skeletal System (Framework & Movement)

* **Composition:** An adult human skeleton consists of **$206\text{ bones}$** joined together to form a rigid framework. **Muscles** are attached to bones to enable movement (walking, running, dancing).
* **3 Major Protective Structures:**
  1. **Skull:** Protects the delicate **brain**.
  2. **Rib Cage:** A cage of curved ribs in the chest that protects the **heart and lungs**.
  3. **Backbone (Vertebral Column / Spine):** Runs down the center of the back and helps the body **stand erect**.
* **Cartilage:** Soft, flexible bone-like tissue present in the **external ear (pinna)** and the **front tip of the nose**.
* **Bone Care:** Eat calcium- and protein-rich foods (**milk, cheese, eggs**), play outdoors in **early morning sunlight** (for Vitamin D), and practice daily exercise/yoga. Doctors use **X-ray images** to examine fractured bones.

#### 2. The Digestive System (Breaking Down Food)

* **Definition of Digestion:** The process of converting complex, insoluble food materials into simple, soluble, and absorbable nutrients by the action of digestive enzymes.

| Organ in Order of Food Pathway | Specific Function in Digestion                                                                                                                                   |
|:------------------------------ |:---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Mouth (Oral Cavity)**     | Teeth cut, chew, and grind food into small pieces; **saliva** mixes with food to soften it and begins starch digestion (*nearly half of digestion starts here*). |
| **2. Oesophagus (Food Pipe)**  | A muscular tube that carries swallowed food smoothly from the mouth down into the stomach.                                                                       |
| **3. Stomach**                 | A muscular bag where food is churned and mixed with **digestive juices** and acid (assisted by juices from the **Liver** and **Pancreas**).                      |
| **4. Small Intestine**         | A **coiled tube $6\text{ meters}$ long** where digestion is completed and **digested nutrients are absorbed into the blood**.                                    |
| **5. Large Intestine**         | Absorbs **excess water** and salts from the remaining undigested food.                                                                                           |
| **6. Rectum & Anus**           | Temporarily stores solid undigested waste (faeces) and expels it out of the body through the **anus**.                                                           |

* **Healthy Eating Rules:** Chew food slowly and thoroughly; drink water **before** meals (not during meals) so food moves smoothly down the food pipe; eat fresh homemade food and avoid junk/street food.

#### 3. The Respiratory System (Breathing & Energy Release)

* **Main Organs:** **Nose (Nasal Cavity) $\rightarrow$ Pharynx $\rightarrow$ Windpipe (Trachea) $\rightarrow$ Lungs (Right Lung and Left Lung)**, resting on a muscular sheet called the **Diaphragm**.
* **Mechanism of Breathing:**
  * **Inspiration (Inhalation):** Breathing **in** oxygen-rich air; the **lungs enlarge (expand)**.
  * **Expiration (Exhalation):** Breathing **out** air rich in **carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) and water vapour**; the **lungs constrict (shrink)**.
* **Cellular Respiration:** Oxygen from the inhaled air passes from the spongy lungs into the blood, reaches every cell in the body, and **breaks down digested food to release energy**.

#### 4. The Circulatory System (Transport System)

* **Main Parts:** **Heart, Blood, and Blood Vessels**.
* **The Heart:**
  * Located in the middle of the chest (slightly tilted left) and works continuously like a motor pump.
  * Divided into **$4\text{ chambers}$**: the upper two small chambers are called **Atria**, and the lower two large chambers are called **Ventricles**.
  * Produces a rhythmic **"lub-dub"** heartbeat sound, which doctors listen to using an instrument called a **Stethoscope**.
* **Blood Vessels ($2\text{ Types}$):**
  1. **Arteries:** Carry **oxygenated (pure) blood** away from the heart to all parts of the body (*Exception:* **Pulmonary arteries** carry deoxygenated blood from the heart to the lungs).
  2. **Veins:** Carry **deoxygenated (impure) blood** from all body parts back to the heart (*Exception:* **Pulmonary veins** carry oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart).
* **Blood & Haemoglobin:**
  * **Functions of Blood:** Transports oxygen and nutrients to cells, carries away waste products like $\text{CO}_2$, regulates body temperature, and fights disease-causing germs.
  * **3 Types of Blood Cells:**
    1. **Red Blood Corpuscles (RBC)**
    2. **White Blood Corpuscles (WBC)** (fight germs)
    3. **Blood Platelets** (help blood clot)
  * **Haemoglobin & Anaemia:** Blood is red due to the iron-rich pigment **haemoglobin**. Deficiency of haemoglobin causes **anaemia** (weakness). Eating **peanut-jaggery bars (*Palli Chikki*—supplied in AP school Mid-Day Meals)**, green leafy vegetables, groundnuts, eggs, and milk boosts haemoglobin.

#### 5. The Excretory System (Waste Removal & Purification)

* **Definition:** The organ system that filters and removes toxic, unnecessary waste products from body fluids.
* **Major Excretory Organs:**
  1. **Kidneys:** A pair of **bean-shaped organs** located in the abdominal cavity, one on either side of the spine. They filter blood to remove nitrogenous waste and excess water as **urine** (Pathway: *Kidneys $\rightarrow$ Ureters $\rightarrow$ Urinary Bladder $\rightarrow$ Bladder Neck/Urethra*).
  2. **Lungs:** Excrete gaseous waste (**carbon dioxide** and water vapour) during exhalation.
  3. **Skin:** The largest visible excretory organ; contains **sweat glands** that excrete excess water and salts as **sweat** through tiny **sweat pores**.
  4. **Liver:** Also helps break down toxins in the blood.
* **Care:** Drink plenty of clean water and eat fiber-rich foods to flush out body wastes.

#### 6. The Nervous System (Master Control & Communication)

* **Main Parts:** **Brain, Spinal Cord, and Nerves**.

* **How It Works:** Works faster than a computer. A network of **nerves** connects every part of the body and the **$5\text{ sense organs}$** (eyes, ears, nose, tongue, skin) to the **brain** via the **spinal cord**.

* When sense organs detect a stimulus (e.g., hearing a loud bus horn or touching something hot), nerves instantly transmit the message to the brain, and the brain sends back instructions to the muscles to react immediately.

* **Chapter 4 Keywords:** *organ, organ system, skull, ribs, bones, digestion, muscle, heart, blood, brain, nerves, lungs, kidney.*

## Chapter 5: Agriculture

### 1. The 11 Stages of Paddy Cultivation ("Field to Plate")

In Andhra Pradesh, **rice** is the staple food obtained from **paddy**, and about **$62\%$ of the state's population** depends on agriculture. Producing just **$1\text{ kilogram}$ of rice requires $3,000\text{ to }5,000\text{ litres}$ of water**.

| Stage No. | Stage Name                | What the Farmer Does                                                                                                  |
|:--------- |:------------------------- |:--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1**     | **Ploughing the field**   | Loosens and turns the soil using bullock-drawn ploughs or tractors.                                                   |
| **2**     | **Leveling**              | Floods the field with water and levels the muddy soil evenly using a leveler.                                         |
| **3**     | **Transplantation**       | Pulls out young paddy seedlings (*Naru*) grown in a nursery bed and transplants them in rows in the flooded field.    |
| **4**     | **Watering (Irrigation)** | Maintains standing water in the field regularly (since paddy is a **water-logged crop**).                             |
| **5**     | **Fertilizing**           | Applies farmyard manure, compost, or fertilizers so plants get soil nutrients for high yield.                         |
| **6**     | **Crop Protection**       | Protects growing plants from weeds, pests, insects, birds, and diseases using scarecrows, fencing, or bio-pesticides. |
| **7**     | **Harvesting**            | Cuts the golden, ripened paddy crop using sickles or harvesting machines.                                             |
| **8**     | **Threshing**             | Sun-dries the harvested crop for a few days and beats/separates the paddy grains from the straw stalks.               |
| **9**     | **Winnowing**             | Drops the threshed grain from a height in the wind so lighter husk and chaff blow away, leaving clean grain.          |
| **10**    | **Storing**               | Packs dried paddy grains into gunny bags and stores them safely in godowns/warehouses.                                |
| **11**    | **Milling**               | Sends paddy to a rice mill where the outer husk is removed to produce edible **rice**.                                |

### 2. Four Methods of Irrigation

**Irrigation** is the artificial supply of water to agricultural fields when rainfall is insufficient.

1. **Field Irrigation:** Flooding the entire field with water (used for water-intensive crops like paddy).
2. **Furrow Irrigation:** Digging parallel trenches (**furrows**) and ridges in the field and supplying water through the channels between crop rows.
3. **Sprinkler Irrigation:** Spraying water into the air through rotating nozzles so it falls on crops like artificial rain (**water-saving method**).
4. **Drip Irrigation:** Supplying water through a network of pipes with tiny holes so water drips **drop-by-drop directly onto the roots** of plants (**best water-conserving method** for dry/scarce water regions).

### 3. Traditional vs. Modern Agricultural Tools

| Traditional Agricultural Tools                                                             | Modern Agricultural Machines                                                        |
|:------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |:----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. **Wooden Plough (*Nagali*)** – pulled by bullocks to till soil.                         | 1. **Tractor** – ploughs, levels, and transports heavy loads quickly.               |
| 2. **Sickle (*Kodavali*)** – hand tool for harvesting crops.                               | 2. **Paddy Transplanter** – machine that transplants paddy seedlings in rows.       |
| 3. **Grape Hoe (*Guddali*) & Spade (*Para*)** – for digging soil and making bunds/furrows. | 3. **Paddy Cutting / Combine Harvester Machine** – cuts and threshes paddy rapidly. |
| 4. **Hand Rake** – for gathering straw, weeds, and leveling soil.                          |                                                                                     |

### 4. Manures, Organic Farming (ZBNF), and Crop Protection

* **Natural Manure vs. Chemical Fertilizers:**
  * Chemical fertilizers and synthetic pesticides leave toxic residues in the soil, destroy soil fertility, kill friendly organisms, and enter the food chain—causing deadly human diseases like **cancer**.
  * **Organic Farming / Zero Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF):** A sustainable farming method that avoids all synthetic chemicals and uses natural inputs like **cow dung, cattle urine, vermicompost, plant remains, oil cakes, and biological wastes**.
  * *Benefits of Organic Farming:* Reduces soil/water pollution, conserves water, prevents soil erosion, increases natural soil fertility, and uses less energy.

### 5. Life Cycles of Farm Organisms (Butterfly, Frog, and Plant)

1. **Life Cycle of a Butterfly / Moth ($4\text{ Stages}$):**
   $$\text{Egg} \rightarrow \text{Caterpillar (Larva)} \rightarrow \text{Pupa (Chrysalis)} \rightarrow \text{Imago (Adult Butterfly/Moth)}$$
   * *Role in Agriculture:* While the **caterpillar** stage eats leaves and damages crops, adult **butterflies and honeybees** are vital friends of the farmer because they perform **pollination** (helping flowers turn into fruits and seeds).
2. **Life Cycle of a Frog ($4\text{ Stages}$):**
   $$\text{Eggs (Spawn)} \rightarrow \text{Tadpole (fish-like)} \rightarrow \text{Tadpole with legs} \rightarrow \text{Young Frog} \rightarrow \text{Adult Frog}$$
   * *Role in Agriculture:* Young **tadpoles** swim in paddy-field water and **eat algae to keep the water clean**. **Adult frogs** eat harmful crop-destroying **insects and pests**.
   * **Bio-Indicators:** Both **frogs and butterflies** have sensitive bodies and act as **indicators of environmental damage**; excessive pesticide spraying wipes them out and breaks the food chain.
3. **Life Cycle of a Rice Plant ($6\text{ Stages}$):**
   $$\text{Seed} \rightarrow \text{Germination} \rightarrow \text{Sprout} \rightarrow \text{Young Plant} \rightarrow \text{Plant with Flowers} \rightarrow \text{Plant with Fruit (Grain)}$$

### 6. Major Crops & Millets of Andhra Pradesh

* **India's Global Rank:** India is the **$2^{\text{nd}}\text{ largest producer}$** of rice, wheat, and cereals in the world.

* **Dam:** A massive barrier constructed across a river to hold back flowing water and raise its level, forming a large artificial lake called a **reservoir**.

  4. **Vamsadhara River** (flows through Srikakulam; merges into the Bay of Bengal)

### 4. River Pollution, Floods, and Droughts

* **Causes of River Pollution ("Tragedy of Rivers"):** Releasing toxic chemical effluents from factories/sugar industries, dairy farm wastes, agricultural chemical runoff, and **ash/waste from thermal power stations** directly into the river.

* **Floods:**
  
  * *Causes:* Heavy torrential rains in the river's catchment area, opening dam spillway gates when reservoirs reach full capacity, and **encroaching riverbeds/catchment canals with illegal buildings**.
  * *Historic Floods:*
    * **$2009\text{ Kurnool Flood}$:** The **Tungabhadra river** flooded Kurnool city, submerging neighbourhoods under **$30\text{ feet}$ of water** for more than $3\text{ days}$.
    * **$2019\text{ Krishna River Flood}$:** Submerged several *Lanka* (river island) villages (such as *Pamullanka* in Thotlavalluru mandal, Krishna district). Affected villagers are rescued and housed in **Government Flood Rehabilitation Centres**.

* **Droughts:** Occur when monsoon rains fail or are severely delayed (common in **Anantapur district**), leaving agricultural lands parched and forcing farm families to migrate.

## Chapter 7: Who Serves Us?

### 1. Three Levels of Government in India

To provide civic amenities (roads, water, schools, hospitals, electricity, security) to all citizens, India has a democratic government functioning at **$3\text{ levels}$**:

1. **Local Self-Government** (Villages, Mandals, Districts, Towns, and Cities)
2. **State Government** (Controls the entire State)
3. **Central / Union Government** (Controls the entire Nation)

## Chapter 11: Earth to Space

### 1. Shape of the Earth

* **Why the Earth Looks Flat to Us:** Just as a small broken shard of a large round clay pot appears flat, the small portion of the Earth we stand on looks flat because the Earth is enormous. In reality, **the Earth is round (spherical)**.
* **2 Scientific Proofs that the Earth is Round:**
  1. **Ferdinand Magellan's Circumnavigation:** Long ago, a Portuguese sailor named **Ferdinand Magellan** and his crew sailed continuously in one direction across the oceans for years and returned to the exact place from where they had started.
  2. **Satellite Photographs from Space:** Photographs of the Earth captured from outer space by artificial satellites clearly show its round, spherical shape.

### 2. The Globe, Latitudes, and Longitudes

* **Globe:** A small three-dimensional ($3\text{D}$) spherical model of the Earth used to study the exact locations of continents, oceans, and countries.
* **Grid of Imaginary Lines on the Globe:**

| Feature                    | Latitudes                                                                                                                       | Longitudes                                                                                                                          |
|:-------------------------- |:------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |:----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Direction Drawn**        | Imaginary **horizontal** circular lines drawn from East to West around the globe.                                               | Imaginary **vertical** semicircle lines drawn from the North Pole to the South Pole.                                                |
| **Central $0^\circ$ Line** | **The Equator ($0^\circ$ Latitude):** Divides the Earth horizontally into two equal halves (Northern and Southern Hemispheres). | **The Prime Meridian ($0^\circ$ Longitude):** Divides the Earth vertically into two equal halves (Eastern and Western Hemispheres). |

### 3. Movements of the Earth: Rotation vs. Revolution

| Feature          | Rotation of the Earth                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Revolution of the Earth                                                                   |
|:---------------- |:-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |:----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Definition**   | The spinning movement of the Earth on its own tilted **imaginary axis** (like a spinning top).                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | The movement of the Earth around the **Sun** in a fixed orbit while rotating on its axis. |
| **Time Taken**   | **$24\text{ hours}$ ($1\text{ Day}$)**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | **About $365\text{ days}$ ($1\text{ Year}$)**                                             |
| **Main Effects** | 1. Causes **Day and Night** (the half facing the Sun has day; the half facing away has night).<br>2. Causes different countries across the world to have **different local clock times** (e.g., when it is $10{:}13\text{ AM}$ in Mumbai, it is $12{:}43\text{ AM}$ at night in New York and $1{:}43\text{ PM}$ in Tokyo). | Causes the **change of Seasons** (Summer, Rainy, Autumn, Winter).                         |

### 4. The Solar System & Natural Satellites

* **The Solar System:** Consists of the **Sun** at the center and all celestial bodies that revolve around it—**$8\text{ Planets}$, Natural Satellites (Moons), Asteroids, and Comets**. We observe distant celestial bodies using an **Astronomical Telescope**.
* **The Sun:** A giant star at the center of our solar system that provides **heat and light** essential for life on Earth.
* **The 8 Planets in Sequential Order from the Sun:**
  *(Memory Aid: "**M**y **V**ery **E**ducated **M**other **J**ust **S**erved **U**s **N**oodles")*
  1. **Mercury** — Nearest planet to the Sun and smallest planet.
  2. **Venus** — Hottest and brightest planet.
  3. **Earth** — **$3^{\text{rd}}\text{ planet}$** from the Sun; the only known planet with liquid water, oxygen, and life.
  4. **Mars** — The Red Planet ($4^{\text{th}}$ planet).
  5. **Jupiter** — Largest planet in the solar system ($5^{\text{th}}$ planet).
  6. **Saturn** — $6^{\text{th}}$ planet, famous for its bright rings.
  7. **Uranus** — $7^{\text{th}}$ planet.
  8. **Neptune** — **$8^{\text{th}}$ and farthest planet** from the Sun.
* **Satellite:** A celestial body that revolves around a planet.
* **The Moon:** The **only natural satellite of the Earth**. It takes **$27\text{ days}$** to complete one revolution around the Earth.

### 5. Artificial Satellites & India's Space Achievements

* **Artificial Satellite:** A man-made scientific spacecraft launched into space orbit around the Earth (or Moon/Mars) using powerful **rockets**.

* **Uses of Artificial Satellites:** Enable mobile phone calls, live TV broadcasting, internet and online banking, weather forecasting (**meteorology**), cyclone tracking, **oceanography**, and surveying mineral/forest resources.

* **Key Indian Space Milestones to Memorize:**
  
  1. **Aryabhata ($1975$):** India's **first artificial satellite**, launched in the year **$1975$**.
  2. **Chandrayaan-2 ($2019$):**
     * India’s second lunar exploration mission to the Moon (following *Chandrayaan-1*).
     * **Launch Date:** **$22^{\text{nd}}\text{ July }2019$**.
     * **Launch Vehicle (Rocket):** **GSLV-MkIII-M1** (*Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle Mark III*).
     * **Launch Site:** **Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC), Sriharikota**, SPSR Nellore District, Andhra Pradesh.
     * **3 Modules of Chandrayaan-2:**
       1. **Orbiter** (revolves around the Moon)
       2. **Vikram** (the Moon **Lander**, named after Dr. Vikram Sarabhai)
       3. **Pragyan** (the robotic Moon **Rover**)

* **Chapter 11 Keywords:** *globe, axis, latitudes, longitudes, equator, prime meridian, rotation, revolution, solar system, satellite, artificial satellite, asteroids, comets.*
