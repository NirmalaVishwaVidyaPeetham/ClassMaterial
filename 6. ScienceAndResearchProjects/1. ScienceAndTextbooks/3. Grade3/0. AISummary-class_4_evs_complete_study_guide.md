## <u>Chapter 1: Family</u>

### 1. Family Relationships & Family Tree

* **Family Tree:** A chart that shows the relationships among different members of a family across generations.

* **Paternal Family Members:** Family members related to your **father**. Your father’s parents are your **paternal grandparents**.

* **Maternal Family Members:** Family members related to your **mother**. Your mother’s parents are your **maternal grandparents**.

* **Extended Family:** Aunts, uncles, and their children (cousins/relatives) who may or may not live in the same house but are part of the larger family.

### 2. Changes in a Family

Families do not stay the same forever. Changes happen when members **join** or **leave** a family due to:

1. **Weddings / Marriages:** A new bride or groom joins the family, while another leaves their parental home.

2. **Birth of a Baby:** A newborn baby adds a new member to the family.

3. **Job Promotions & Transfers:** When an employee is promoted or transferred to another town, the whole family may shift (e.g., moving from Ongole to Nellore), or the parent may travel daily while the family stays behind.

4. **Other Reasons:** Moving away for higher studies, business, employment, or the death (expiry) of a family member.
* **Impact:** These changes have led to a decrease in joint families and an **increase in small (nuclear) families**.

### 3. Types of Families

1. **Nuclear Family (Small Family):** Consists of only the mother, father, and their children.

2. **Joint Family:** Consists of grandparents, parents, uncles, aunts, and cousins all living together in the same house.

3. **Single-Parent Family:** Consists of children living with only one parent (either mother or father), often due to the loss of the other parent in an accident or illness. The single parent both earns a living and does household chores.

4. **Grandparent Family:** Children live with and are cared for solely by their grandparents (e.g., when both parents are lost in an accident).

5. **Mother-Dependent Family:** Both parents live in the house, but the mother takes full responsibility for earning and caring for the family because the father is ill or unable to work.

> **Important Social Note:** Addiction to alcohol is injurious to health and disturbs the peace and finances of a family. To protect families, the Government of Andhra Pradesh is taking steps to ban alcohol consumption in a phased manner.

### 4. Home Appliances: Then and Now

* **Common Home Appliances:** Refrigerator (fridge), washing machine, table fan, ceiling fan, television (TV), wet grinder, mixer-grinder (mixie), pressure cooker, air cooler, and air conditioner (AC).

* **Lifestyle Changes (Then vs. Now):**
  
  * **Then:** People did household chores manually (providing good physical exercise) and slept outdoors in verandahs or open yards, enjoying fresh air with fans made of **palm leaves or bamboo**.
  
  * **Now:** People use electrical appliances to make work easier and faster, and sleep indoors for safety under ceiling fans, air coolers, or air conditioners.

* **Disadvantages of Modern Appliances:**
  
  1. High consumption of electricity.
  
  2. Reduced physical exercise for the body.
  
  3. **Environmental Pollution:** Harmful cooling gases leaked from refrigerators and air conditioners pollute the atmosphere and are dangerous.

* **Chapter 1 Keywords:** *nuclear family, joint family, single parent family, mother dependent family, siblings, transfer, promotion, home appliances, family tree.*

## <u>Chapter 2: Green World</u>

### 1. Classification of Plants by Habitat (Dwelling Places)

Plants grow in different regions depending on the **soil** and **climate**. Based on where they live, plants are divided into two main groups:

#### A. Terrestrial Plants (Land Plants)

Plants that grow on land are called terrestrial plants.

* **Plains:** Banyan, peepal, mango, tamarind, and blackberry (*neredu*).

* **Mountains (Hilly/Cold regions):** Tall trees like pine and oak.

* **Deserts (Dry regions):** Cactus, barrel cactus, and golden barrel cactus. They survive with very little water by **storing water in their thick, fleshy stems**.

* **Marshy or Swampy Areas:** **Mangroves** grow in wet, muddy coastal areas.

#### B. Aquatic Plants (Water Plants)

Plants that grow in water are called aquatic plants. Their roots are soft and spongy to help them float.

* **Free-floating plants:** Duckweed and water hyacinth (*Gurrapudekka*).

* **Fixed aquatic plants (roots fixed to the bottom of ponds/lakes):** Water lily and lotus.

* **Submerged aquatic plants (grow completely underwater):** Hydrilla and tape-grass.

### 2. Parts of a Plant

A plant body is divided into two main systems:

1. **The Root System:** The part of the plant that grows **below the soil**.

2. **The Shoot System:** The part of the plant that grows **above the soil** (stem, leaves, buds, flowers, and fruits).

### 3. The Root System: Types and Uses

* **Functions of Roots:** Hold the plant firmly in the soil, absorb water and mineral nutrients from the soil, and store food in certain plants. Roots are generally brown in colour.

* **Depth of Roots:** Big trees like banyan, neem, and tamarind have roots that go very deep into the soil. Smaller plants like rose and jasmine have shallow roots.

| Type of Root     | Description                                                                                             | Examples                                  |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Tap Root**     | Has one thick main root that goes deep into the soil, with several thin side roots growing out from it. | Neem, Tamarind, Mango, Carrot             |
| **Fibrous Root** | Bushy roots where many thin roots of similar size grow like a bunch from the lower end of the stem.     | Paddy (Rice), Jowar, Maize, Grass, Grains |

* **Uses of Roots:**
  
  * **As Food (Storage Roots):** Carrot, beetroot, and radish store food in their roots and are eaten as vegetables.
  
  * **Cooling & Fragrance:** **Camel grass (*Vattiverlu* / Vetiver)** roots are woven into cooling mats for summer and kept as air fresheners in wardrobes.
  
  * **Oils & Repellents:** **Lemon grass** roots are used to make fragrant oils and insect repellents.

### 4. Flowers: Parts and Uses

* **Parts of a Flower (e.g., Hibiscus):**
  
  1. **Bud:** An unopened flower.
  
  2. **Sepal:** The green outer leaf-like part that protects the bud.
  
  3. **Receptacle:** The thickened base of the stem where the flower parts are attached.
  
  4. **Petal:** The bright, colourful part of the flower that attracts insects.
  
  5. **Stamen:** The male reproductive part of the flower.
  
  6. **Pistil:** The female central part of the flower.

* **Uses of Flowers:**
  
  * **Decoration:** Used to make garlands and decorate houses.
  
  * **Medicines:** Hibiscus, Neem, and Tulasi flowers.
  
  * **Perfumes & Beauty Oils:** Rose, Jasmine, Lily, and Lavender (due to their pleasant fragrance).
  
  * **As Food:** Cauliflower is a flower eaten as a vegetable.

### 5. Fruits and Seeds

* Flowers develop and grow into **fruits**, which contain seeds inside them.

* **Seedless Fruits:** Farmers now grow seedless fruits (such as seedless grapes) using **hybrid technology**.

* **Uses of Fruits:**
  
  * Rich source of **vitamins, minerals, and dietary fiber**.
  
  * **Hair Care:** Soap-nuts (*Kunkudu*) and *Shikakai* fruits are used to cleanse hair naturally.
  
  * **Medicinal Value:** Lemon, Neem, and Amla (Indian gooseberry).

> **Special Box – Ugadi Pickle (*Ugadi Pachadi*):**
> A traditional festival dish of Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, and Karnataka made mostly from plant products. It combines **six tastes (*Shadruchulu*)** that symbolically represent the **six emotions of human life**:
> 
> 1. **Sweet (*Theepi*):** Jaggery (Happiness)
> 
> 2. **Sour (*Pulupu*):** Tamarind (Disgust/Unpleasantness)
> 
> 3. **Salty (*Uppu*):** Salt (Fear/Interest in life)
> 
> 4. **Acrid / Astringent (*Vagaru*):** Raw mango / Raw coconut (Surprise/New challenges)
> 
> 5. **Spicy / Pungent (*Karam*):** Green chilli / Pepper (Anger)
> 
> 6. **Bitter (*Chedu*):** Neem flowers (Sadness)

### 6. Photosynthesis

* **Definition:** The process by which green plants prepare their own food using **carbon dioxide (**$\text{CO}_2$**)** from the air, **water (**$\text{H}_2\text{O}$**)** from the soil, and **sunlight**, while releasing **oxygen (**$\text{O}_2$**)** into the air.

* **Chapter 2 Keywords:** *shoot system, root system, tap root, fibrous root, minerals, turmeric, nutrients, garland, aquatic, terrestrial, marshy, swampy, climate.*

## Chapter 3: Animals Around Us

### 1. External Features: Ears and Skin

* **Visibility of Ears:**
  
  * **Visible External Ears:** Elephant (very large ears), rabbit, giraffe, buffalo, cow, deer, dog, cat, mouse, tiger, lion.
  
  * **Invisible External Ears:** Birds (hen, crow, sparrow, duck), snakes, lizards, crocodiles, frogs, and fish.
  
  * *How Birds Hear:* Birds have **tiny holes on both sides of their head**, hidden beneath their feathers, which help them hear sounds.

* **Skin, Hair, and Fur:**
  
  * Animals are identified by the colour, texture, and pattern of hair/fur on their skin (e.g., **black and white stripes** on a zebra, **black spots** on a leopard, orange and black stripes on a tiger).
  
  * **Uses:** Thick fur (wool) of sheep is used to make sweaters and blankets; the skin (leather) of some dead animals is used to make footwear (chappals) and bags.

### 2. Classification Based on Reproduction: Viviparous vs. Oviparous

| Feature           | Viviparous Animals                                              | Oviparous Animals                                                      |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Reproduction**  | Give birth directly to live young ones (babies).                | Lay eggs, incubate them, and hatch their young ones.                   |
| **Ears**          | External ears are **visible**.                                  | External ears are **not visible** (invisible).                         |
| **Skin**          | Have hair or fur on their skin.                                 | Do not have hair on their skin (have feathers, scales, or moist skin). |
| **Feeding Young** | Generally **mammals**; mothers feed milk to their babies.       | Do not produce milk; find insects/grains for hatchlings.               |
| **Examples**      | Cow, Dog, Elephant, Tiger, Human, **Dolphin** (aquatic mammal). | Hen, Crow, Duck, Eagle, Snake, Lizard, Crocodile, Frog, Fish.          |

### 3. Classification Based on Food Habits

| Category       | Diet                                                                | Teeth / Mouth Adaptation                                                                            | Examples                                                                                                   |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Herbivores** | Eat only plants and plant products (grass, leaves, fruits, grains). | Sharp front cutting teeth and flat, strong grinding teeth.                                          | Cow, Buffalo, Goat, Zebra, Deer, Horse, Rabbit, Elephant, Monkey, Squirrel, Kangaroo, Pigeon, Caterpillar. |
| **Carnivores** | Eat only the flesh/meat of other animals.                           | Long, sharp, pointed tearing teeth (canines); carnivorous birds have sharp hooked beaks and talons. | Lion, Tiger, Leopard, Fox, Snake, Lizard, Frog, Eagle, Vulture.                                            |
| **Omnivores**  | Eat **both** plants and animals.                                    | Have sharp cutting teeth, canines, and strong grinding teeth.                                       | Bear, Human beings, Crow, Dog, Cat, Hen, Fish.                                                             |

### 4. Birds: Beaks, Feet, Claws, and Nests

* **Beaks:** Beaks are the jaws of birds. Their shapes (small, long, curved, hooked, flat) depend on the kind of food the bird eats.

* **Feet and Claws:**
  
  * Used for walking, climbing, swimming, and perching on branches.
  
  * **Claws** have long curved nails used for catching/holding prey and for self-defense.

* **Nests of Birds:** Birds build nests to lay eggs and raise chicks. Usually, the **male bird builds the nest** and the **female bird lays eggs** in it.
  
  * **Crow:** Builds its nest high in trees using **twigs and dried leaves**.
  
  * **Weaver Bird (*Gijigadu*):** Weaves a hanging, pot-shaped nest using **grass, leaves, fine twigs, and roots**.
  
  * **Tailor Bird:** Stitches large green leaves together using plant fibers or wool like a needle and thread.
  
  * **Eagle:** Builds a wide platform nest using **large sticks, grass, and plant stalks** on high trees or rocky cliffs.

### 5. Animal Shelters

* **Domestic Animals:** Live in shelters built by humans (e.g., cows in sheds, dogs in kennels).

* **Wild Animals:** Live in natural shelters in forests:
  
  * **Lions and Tigers:** Caves (dens) or thick bushes.
  
  * **Rabbits and Rats:** Burrows (holes dug in the ground).
  
  * **Monkeys and Squirrels:** On trees.
  
  * **Spiders:** Spin their own cobwebs.

### 6. Group Behaviour of Animals

Animals live and move in groups for survival, finding food, saving energy, and defending themselves from enemies.

1. **Migrating Birds (V-Formation):** Birds flying long distances fly in a **'V' shape**. Each bird flies slightly higher than the bird in front of it, which reduces wind resistance (wind speed) and helps the flock **save energy**.

2. **Ants:** Live in underground colonies called **anthills**. An ant colony has strict discipline and division of labour among **queen ants, male ants, and worker ants**. When an ant finds food, it releases a chemical **secretion** on the ground so other ants can smell it and follow in a single line.

3. **Honeybees:** Live in large colonies in a **beehive**. Worker bees build wax honeycombs together, collect nectar, share food, and feed the young bee larvae.

4. **Elephants:** Live in groups called **herds** of about $10$ to $12$ elephants along with their calves for protection. The **oldest (elderly) female elephant** is the leader of the herd; she trumpets in the morning to lead the herd on its daily journey.
* **Chapter 3 Keywords:** *viviparous, oviparous, mammals, pattern, colonies, secretion, migration, protection.*

## Chapter 4: Sense Organs

### 1. Overview of the Body and Five Sense Organs

* **Three Main Parts of the Human Body:** Head, Trunk, and Limbs (hands and legs).

* **Sense Organs:** The five organs that help our body sense and respond to external stimuli:
  
  1. **Eyes** – Sense of Sight (Vision)
  
  2. **Nose** – Sense of Smell (and Breathing)
  
  3. **Ears** – Sense of Hearing (Sound)
  
  4. **Tongue** – Sense of Taste (and Speech)
  
  5. **Skin** – Sense of Touch (Feeling hot, cold, rough, smooth, pain)

. **Extra information:** **Proprioception** is your body's ability to sense its own position, movement, and force in three-dimensional space, often called the body's "sixth sense".

### 2. Detailed Functions, Care, and Animal Facts for Each Sense Organ

#### A. Eyes (Sense of Sight)

* **Protection:** Eyelids and eyelashes naturally protect the eyes from dust and injury.

* **Rules for Eye Care:**
  
  1. Read only in proper light (neither too dim nor too bright). Maintain a distance of $30\text{ cm}$ between your eyes and the book.
  
  2. Watch television from a minimum distance of $6\text{ feet}$.
  
  3. Avoid playing mobile or computer games continuously, as it strains eyesight.
  
  4. If your eyes itch, do not rub them; gently wash them with clean, cool water.
  
  5. Never play with sharp or pointed objects, and never look directly at the Sun or very bright lights.

* **Blindness & Braille Script:** People who cannot see are blind. They walk with the help of a white cane/special stick and read using **Braille script** (a system of raised dots read by touching with the fingers), which was invented by **Louis Braille**.

* **Animal Facts:** Honeybees have $5$ **eyes**. Eagles, vultures, and hawks have extremely sharp eyesight. **Owls** can see clearly in the dark.

#### B. Nose (Sense of Smell)

* **Function:** Helps us smell pleasant and unpleasant odours and breathe air. Tiny **hairs inside the nose** filter out dust particles so clean air enters the lungs.

* **Rules for Nose Care:**
  
  1. Never insert fingers or small objects into the nose.
  
  2. Always breathe through your nose, not through your mouth.
  
  3. If the nose is blocked due to a cold, **inhale steam (vapour)** to clear it.

* **Animal Facts:** Dogs and cats have a very powerful sense of smell. Police train **sniffer dogs** to track criminals, bombs, and hidden weapons. **Elephants** can smell water from a distance of $3\text{ kilometres}$.

#### C. Ears (Sense of Hearing)

* **Function:** Help us hear sounds around us. Inside the ear is a delicate membrane called the **eardrum**, which can be damaged by very loud noises (thunder, firecrackers, loudspeakers, or continuous music on earphones).

* **Rules for Ear Care:**
  
  1. Never insert hairpins, matchsticks, pencils, or slate pencils into the ears.
  
  2. Dry your ears gently with a soft cloth after bathing.
  
  3. Avoid bursting loud firecrackers (they harm ears, frighten animals, and disturb babies and elderly people).
  
  4. Use a few drops of **hydrogen peroxide** (under adult/medical supervision) to safely soften and remove ear wax.

* **Animal Facts:** **Nocturnal animals** (animals active at night) like **owls and bats** have a very sharp sense of hearing. Cats can detect even very faint sounds.

#### D. Tongue (Sense of Taste)

* **Function:** Covered with tiny **taste buds** that detect different tastes (sweet, sour, salty, bitter, spicy) and helps us **speak clearly**.

* **Rules for Tongue & Oral Care:**
  
  1. Clean your tongue daily with a **tongue cleaner** after brushing to prevent bad breath.
  
  2. Brush your teeth **twice a day** (in the morning after waking up and at night before going to bed).
  
  3. Avoid eating food that is too hot or too cold, as it damages taste buds and tooth enamel.

* **Sign Language:** People who cannot speak or hear use **sign language** (hand gestures and expressions) to communicate.

* **Animal Facts:** Frogs, lizards, and chameleons have long, sticky tongues to catch insects. Giraffes have very long tongues. **Snakes** flick their tongues out to **smell** their surroundings.

#### E. Skin (Sense of Touch)

* **Function:** Covers the entire body and is the **largest and most sensitive** of all sense organs. It helps us feel whether objects are hot, cold, rough, smooth, hard, or soft.

* **Personal Hygiene & Skin Care:**
  
  1. Bathe daily with a mild soap (and bathe after playing to wash off sweat, dirt, and germs that cause skin allergies and itching).
  
  2. Keep the skin clean and dry, especially between fingers, toes, knees, elbows, and private parts to prevent fungal infections.
  
  3. Take a head bath at least **twice a week**.
  
  4. Trim fingernails and toenails regularly to prevent germs from entering the body while eating.
  
  5. Wash hands with soap after using the toilet and before and after eating; cover your nose and mouth with a handkerchief when sneezing or coughing.

* **Animal Fact:** A snake's skin is sensitive to vibrations and can sense approaching enemies from a distance.

### 3. Persons with Disabilities (Differently-Abled People)

* **PWD Act – 2016:** Under the *Persons with Disabilities Act (2016)*, individuals who cannot walk, see, hear, or speak (either from birth or due to accidents) are recognized as persons with disabilities (differently-abled).

* **Support & Respect:** We must never tease, bully, or call them by nicknames. The government supports them with financial aid, scholarships, **ramps, crutches, callipers, tricycles, and wheelchairs**.

* **Inspiring Role Models:**
  
  * **Sudha Chandran:** A world-famous classical dancer and actress who lost her leg in an accident and achieved great fame dancing with an artificial prosthetic leg (*Jaipur foot*).
  
  * **Ravindra Jain:** Born blind, he became a legendary music composer and singer in Indian cinema through sheer talent and hard work.
  
  * **Helen Keller:** Blind and deaf since early childhood, she became a world-renowned author and speaker.

### 4. Good Touch and Bad Touch (Child Safety)

* **Good Touch:** Physical contact that makes you feel safe, loved, and comfortable (e.g., a mother's hug or a pat on the back for doing well).

* **Bad Touch:** Any physical contact that makes you feel uncomfortable, scared, or ashamed.

* **Private Parts:** **Lips, chest, between the legs, and bottom (seat).** No one has the right to look at, touch, or talk about your private parts, or ask you to look at or touch theirs.

* **Warning Tricks Used by Abusers:**
  
  * Falsely claiming your parent is admitted to the hospital and asking you to come along.
  
  * Tempting you with chocolates, sweets, gifts, or money.
  
  * Taking you to a lonely place, touching you unnecessarily, showing uncomfortable photos/videos on a phone, forcing a kiss, or offering to dress/undress you.

* **What to Do If Someone Touches You Inappropriately:**
  
  1. **Say "NO!" or "Don't touch!"** loudly and firmly.
  
  2. **Run away** and leave the spot immediately.
  
  3. **Scream for help** and report everything to trusted adults (parents or teachers).
  
  4. **Never blame yourself**—you did nothing wrong.

* **Emergency Helpline:** **CHILD LINE** $1098$ is India's national, $24 \times 7$ **free emergency phone service** for children in need of care and protection.

* **Signs a Friend May Be Experiencing Bad Touch:** Staying alone and sad, avoiding friends, trying to harm themselves, showing sudden fear of a specific person, refusing to go anywhere alone, or losing interest in studies.

## Chapter 5: Eat Together

### 1. Importance of Eating Together & Regular Meals

* **Occasions for Eating Together:** Family weddings, festivals, community functions, picnics, and school lunch breaks. Eating together builds joy, unity, sharing, and friendship.

* **Why Breakfast Matters:** Food gives our body energy to grow, play, and study. Skipping breakfast causes weakness, dizziness, fainting during school assembly, and poor concentration in class.

### 2. The Mid-Day Meal Scheme

* **Purpose:** Many poor children come to school hungry. To ensure every child receives healthy, nutritious food and attends school regularly, the Government introduced the **Mid-Day Meal Scheme** as a right of every child.

* **Typical Menu Items:** Rice, sambar with vegetables, boiled eggs, vegetable rice, lemon rice, and peanut *chikki* (jaggery brittle).

* **Good Habits:** Eat all the vegetables and curry leaves in your food for nutrition, do not spill food while eating, and never leave food wasted on your plate.

### 3. Avoiding Food Wastage & Saying "No" to Plastic

* **Food & Water Wastage:** Large amounts of food and drinking water are wasted at weddings, functions, and homes when cooked in excess or left unfinished on plates and in glasses.

* **Harms of Using Plastic Plates, Glasses, and Covers:**
  
  1. **Health Hazard:** When hot food or water is served or stored in plastic, microscopic plastic particles leach into the food and enter our body, causing serious illnesses.
  
  2. **Soil Pollution:** Thrown-away plastic does not decay and pollutes the soil.
  
  3. **Harm to Animals:** Stray cows and buffaloes swallow plastic bags mixed with leftover food and fall severely sick or die.
  
  4. **Air Pollution:** Burning plastic releases toxic smoke into the air.

* **Eco-Friendly Alternatives to Plastic:**
  
  * Use steel plates and glasses, cloth or jute bags, and traditional biodegradable leaf plates (*Vistaraku*) stitched from **banyan (*Marri*), almond (*Badam*), or flame-of-the-forest (*Moduga*) leaves**.
  
  * **Reuse (DIY):** Empty PET plastic water bottles can be cut and decorated to make **pen stands** or **hanging flower pots** (e.g., for growing a money plant in water, changing the water weekly so mosquitoes do not breed).

### 4. Preservation of Food

When food is kept uncovered or stored improperly in moisture, a white cottony layer of **fungus** or bacteria grows on it and spoils it. We preserve food using different methods:

| Method of Preservation               | How It Works                                                                                                                                                                                              | Food Items Preserved                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Pickling (Salt & Oil)**            | **Salt** acts as a natural preservative, and **oil** forms a protective layer that prevents air, bacteria, and fungus from spoiling the food.                                                             | Mango pickle, Lemon pickle, Tomato pickle, Gongura pickle, Meat/Fish pickles.                          |
| **Drying in Sunlight (Dehydration)** | Removes moisture from food so microbes cannot grow.                                                                                                                                                       | Fryums (*Vadiyalu / Orugulu*), Salted vegetables (*Challamirapakayalu*), Papad, Dry fish, Food grains. |
| **Sugaring (Sugar Syrup)**           | High concentration of sugar prevents bacterial growth.                                                                                                                                                    | Fruit Jams, Jellies, Murabba.                                                                          |
| **Freezing & Refrigeration**         | Very low temperatures (cooling effect) keep microbes inactive for several days.                                                                                                                           | Fresh vegetables, fruits, milk, curd, meat, and fish.                                                  |
| **Dry Grain Storage**                | Harvested grains are dried thoroughly in the sun and stored with **dried neem leaves** inside airtight **tin, steel, aluminium, or bamboo** containers to protect against rats, mice, insects, and fungi. | Rice, Wheat, Pulses (Grams/Dals), and Oil seeds (Groundnuts).                                          |

> **Traditional Recipe – Bengal Gram Powder (*Kandi Podi*):** Made by roasting and grinding **Bengal gram (Toor dal), red chillies, cumin seeds (*Jeelakarra*), and salt**.

### 5. From Field to Plate (People Behind Our Food)

Every single grain of food reaches our plate through the hard work of many professionals and artisans:

* **Farmer & Agricultural Labourers:** Plough the soil, sow seeds, water crops, and harvest grain.

* **Blacksmith (*Kammari*):** Makes iron farming tools like the **sickle (*Kodavali*)**.

* **Carpenter (*Vadrangi*):** Makes wooden farming tools like the **plough (*Nagali*)** and bullock cart.

* **Miller:** Hulls paddy into rice and grinds grains into flour at the mill.

* **Vendors / Merchants:** Transport and sell food grains and vegetables in the market.

## Chapter 6: Water

### 1. Sources of Water & Village Tanks (*Cheruvu*)

* **Water Resources:** Rain, ponds, village tanks (*cheruvulu*), canals, rivers, open wells, and borewells (which draw underground water).

* **Case Study – Yathalavanka Cheruvu:** Located in **Neetiguntlapalli village, Punganoor mandal, Chittoor district**. Originally a small pond, villagers collectively expanded it into a large tank to store rainwater.

* **How a Village Tank is Built:**
  
  1. Villagers identify a source of floodwater and a low-lying area.
  
  2. They dig the basin and build strong raised mud and stone walls around it called **tankbunds (embankments)** to hold water.
  
  3. **Inflow canals** are dug to guide rainwater from catchment areas into the tank.
  
  4. **Outlets (*Marava / Thoomu*)** are built on both sides of the embankment to safely release excess water during heavy floods and supply water to fields.

* **Interlinked Tanks & River-Fed Tanks:**
  
  * In **Anantapur district**, tanks like **Bukkapatnam** and **Dharmavaram** are interlinked by canals so excess rainwater flows from one tank to another.
  
  * In **Krishna, East Godavari, and West Godavari districts**, canals are dug to fill village tanks directly with river water.

* **Benefits of a Village Tank:**
  
  1. Provides water for **irrigating** large areas of agricultural land.
  
  2. Serves as a drinking water source for cattle and villagers.
  
  3. **Recharges Groundwater:** Percolation of tank water raises the underground water table so wells and borewells do not dry up in summer.

### 2. Tank Pollution, Drying Up, and Management

* **How Tanks Get Polluted:**
  
  * People bathing, washing clothes, washing vehicles, and bathing cattle inside the tank.
  
  * Throwing household garbage and dead animal carcasses into the water, or defecating near tank bunds.
  
  * Immersing festival idols painted with toxic chemicals (which kills fish and aquatic plants).
  
  * Releasing household sewage and **chemical effluents** from factories into the tank.

* **Water-Borne Diseases:** Drinking untreated, polluted tank water directly causes dangerous diseases like **typhoid, cholera, and diarrhea**. (Always **boil** water before drinking to kill harmful micro-organisms).

* **Why Tanks Dry Up:**
  
  * Low rainfall and drought.
  
  * Human greed: Encroaching and occupying tank beds and inflow channels to build houses or farms, which blocks rainwater from entering the tank.
  
  * Overgrowth of invasive weeds like **Eichhornia (*Gurrapudekka* / water hyacinth)** and algae.

* **Tank Management (Water User's Society):**
  
  * The Government forms a committee of local farmers called the **Water User's Society** (*Neeti Sangham*) to maintain village tanks.
  
  * They clear weeds from tanks and canals and dig out the fertile **silt (soil deposits)** from the tank bed during summer to spread on their crop fields. Desilting both **fertilizes the fields** and **increases the water-holding depth of the tank**.

### 3. Safe Drinking Water Scheme (Water Treatment Plant)

In rural areas, the **Village Panchayat (Gram Panchayat)** supplies safe drinking water from overhead tanks after purifying pond/river water through $5$ **stages**:

1. **Sedimentation Tank:** Water is pumped from a river or tank into a large sedimentation tank and left undisturbed. Heavy sand and mud settle to the bottom, and floating twigs and leaves are removed.

2. **Filtration Tank:** Water passes through layers of sand and gravel to filter out smaller suspended dirt particles.

3. **Chlorination Tank:** **Bleaching powder (chlorine)** is added to the filtered water. This process, called **chlorination**, kills harmful germs and bacteria.

4. **Overhead Storage Tank:** The purified, chlorinated water is pumped up into tall overhead tanks.

5. **Home Supply:** Safe drinking water reaches household taps through a network of underground pipes.

### 4. The Water Cycle (Journey of a Raindrop)

The continuous circulation of water from the Earth's surface into the atmosphere and back to the Earth as rain is called the **Water Cycle**. It involves three main processes:

1. **Evaporation:** The heat of the Sun turns liquid water in oceans, rivers, tanks, and ponds into **water vapour** (gas), which rises into the sky.

2. **Condensation:** High up in the sky, cool air cools the rising water vapour, turning it back into tiny liquid water droplets that gather to form **clouds**.

3. **Precipitation (Rain):** When the water droplets in the clouds become heavy, they fall back to the Earth as **rain**.

### 5. Properties of Water (Soluble vs. Insoluble Substances)

* Water has the property of dissolving many substances. In fact, groundwater tastes sweet, salty, or bland depending on the soil minerals dissolved in it.
  
  * **Soluble in Water (Dissolve completely):** Sugar, Salt, Milk (changes water colour to white).
  
  * **Insoluble in Water (Do not dissolve):** Oil (floats on top), Bengal gram/Dal (settles at the bottom), Flour (makes water cloudy and settles).

* **The** $6$**-Step Process of Making Pots:**
  
  1. Collects and brings fine **clay** from fields or tank beds.

  4. Gently taps the pot with a flat **wooden paddle/plate** to give it the proper shape and thinness.
  
  5. Dries the shaped pots **first in the shade** and **then under the sun**.
  
  6. Bakes the sun-dried pots in a hot furnace called a **kiln (*ava*)** to make them hard and durable.

## Chapter 9: Communications

### 1. What is Communication?

* **Definition:** The process of expressing and sharing ideas, feelings, messages, information, or news from one person to another.

* **Two Main Types of Communication:**
  
  1. **One-to-One (Personal) Communication:** Sharing messages between two individuals or a small group (e.g., letters, phone calls, SMS, e-mail).
  
  2. **Mass Communication:** Sharing news and information with a very large population at the same time (e.g., newspapers, radio, television, social media).

### 2. Postal Services & The Journey of a Letter

* **Types of Postal Stationery:** Postcard (e.g., *Meghdoot* Post Card), Inland Letter Card, and Stamped Envelope.

* **6 Essential Parts of a Postal Address:**
  
  1. Name of the Receiver
  
  2. House / Door Number and Street
  
  3. Name of the Village or Town
  
  4. Name of the Mandal
  
  5. Name of the District
  
  6. **PIN Code (Postal Index Number):** A unique $6$-digit number allotted to every post office in India that helps postal workers identify the exact location and sort mail quickly (e.g., $532460$).

* **The** $6$**-Step Journey of a Letter:**
  
  1. Write the letter with the complete address and PIN code.
  
  2. Drop the letter into the local red **post box**.
  
  3. The postman collects the letters and takes them to the post office, where they are stamped and **sorted according to destination addresses**.
  
  4. Mail bags are transported to the destination town/city by **train, bus, aeroplane, or postal van**.
  
  5. The destination post office sorts the letters street-wise.
  
  6. The delivery **postman** brings the letter to the receiver's home.

> **Activity – Toy Telephone:** Made using two empty tin cans/paper cups with a small hole punched at the bottom of each, connected tightly by a long piece of string. Sound travels as vibrations along the tight string!

### 3. Modern Instant One-to-One Communication

1. **Mobile Phones (Smartphones):**
   
   * Enable instant voice calls, SMS (Short Message Service), and audio/video conference calls anywhere.
   
   * Smartphone apps allow us to play games, use banking services, and book bus/train/flight tickets from home.
   
   * **Good Telephone Manners (Phone Etiquette):**
     
     * Greet politely with *"Hello"* or *"Good morning"* and tell your name.
     
     * Say *"Please hold on"* or *"May I call back later?"* when busy.
     
     * Cover the mouthpiece with a handkerchief if you need to cough or sneeze.
     
     * Say *"Sorry, I cannot hear you clearly"* if the network is poor.
     
     * End the call politely with *"Thank you, nice talking to you."*

2. **E-Mail (Electronic Mail):**
   
   * One of the fastest and cheapest modern ways to send written messages, documents, photos, and videos from one computer, laptop, or smartphone to another anywhere in the world using the **Internet**.

### 4. Mass Communication & Social Media

* **Newspaper (Print Media):** A daily printed publication containing local and world news, opinion articles, sports features, and advertisements.

* **Radio (Audio Media):** Broadcasts spoken news, music, educational programmes, and debates. It is crucial for broadcasting **hour-to-hour cyclone and flood warnings** to fishermen and villagers even when electricity fails.

* **Television / TV (Audio-Visual Media):** Allows people to both **see and hear** live news, educational programmes, sports, and movies.

* **Social Media:** Internet-based platforms and apps (such as WhatsApp, Facebook, YouTube, Instagram, and Twitter/X) used on computers and smartphones to create and share ideas, videos, and messages globally.

### 5. Movements of the Earth, Moon Phases, and Stars

| Feature        | Rotation of the Earth                                                                                                                                                              | Revolution of the Earth                                       |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Definition** | The spinning movement of the Earth on its own **axis**.                                                                                                                            | The movement of the Earth around the **Sun** in a fixed path. |
| **Time Taken** | $24\text{ hours}$ ($1\text{ day}$).                                                                                                                                                | $365\frac{1}{4}\text{ days}$ ($1\text{ year}$).               |
| **Effect**     | **Causes Day and Night.** The half of the Earth facing the Sun has day, while the half away from the Sun has night (which is why when it is day in India, it is night in America). | Causes changes in seasons and marks a calendar year.          |

* **Phases of the Moon:** The sunlit shape of the Moon appears to change night after night over a $15$-day waxing and $15$-day waning cycle. These changing shapes are called the **phases of the moon**.
  
  * **Full Moon Day (*Pournami*):** The night when the Moon appears completely round and bright like a silver plate.
  
  * **No Moon / New Moon Day (*Amavasya*):** The night when the Moon is not visible in the sky at all.

* **Constellation:** A group of stars in the night sky that appear to form a recognizable pattern or picture.
