# Comprehensive Study Guide: Section 5 – The Ancient Poets and the Origin of Priesthood
**From William Blake’s *The Marriage of Heaven and Hell* (Plate 11)**

*(Note on Section 5: Immediately following the "Proverbs of Hell," Blake presents this brief, densely argued prose manifesto on Plate 11. Here, he returns to the historical theme first introduced in "The Argument"—how the "villain" usurped the garden planted by the "just man"—and explains the exact psychological and historical process by which joyful poetic imagination was hijacked and hardened into authoritarian religion.)*

---

## Sentence-by-Sentence Breakdown

### Sentence 1
> **Original Text:**  
> "The ancient poets animated all sensible objects with Gods or Geniuses, calling them by the names and adorning them with properties of woods, rivers, mountains, lakes, cities, nations, and whatever their enlarged and numerous senses could perceive."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**  
  *Translation:* The earliest poets brought every physical object in the natural and human world to spiritual life by recognizing within them living "Gods" or guardian spirits ("Geniuses"), naming them and clothing them with the unique qualities of forests, rivers, mountains, lakes, cities, and nations—as well as every other reality that their expanded, more-than-fivefold senses were able to perceive.  
  *Implied Meaning:* Blake engages eighteenth-century theories of mythology and the origins of language (such as those of Giambattista Vico and Jean-Jacques Rousseau). While Enlightenment rationalists claimed that ancient peoples invented gods out of primitive fear and ignorance of natural science, Blake argues the exact opposite: the ancient bards possessed **"enlarged and numerous senses"** (perception not yet shrunk down to the narrow lock of the five physical senses). When these poets looked at a river, a forest, or a mountain, they did not see dead, mechanical matter; through imaginative empathy, they "animated" (gave soul and life to) nature, recognizing the unique spiritual character ("Genius") of every living environment.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**  
  Long, long ago, the very first storytellers and **ancient poets** looked at the world with giant, wide-awake imaginations! Their senses were much bigger than just looking at things like plain rocks or dirt. When they looked at a tall mountain, a rushing river, a deep green forest, or a bustling city, they felt that each place was alive with its own special spirit or character (which they called a **"God"** or a **"Genius"**). So they gave those rivers, woods, and mountains wonderful names and told stories about their personalities to celebrate how alive the world was!

---

### Sentence 2
> **Original Text:**  
> "And particularly they studied the Genius of each city and country, placing it under its mental deity."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**  
  *Translation:* In particular, these ancient poets closely observed the distinct cultural spirit and character ("the Genius") of every individual city and nation, symbolically representing each community under its own guardian "mental deity."  
  *Implied Meaning:* In classical antiquity, every place and community had its *genius loci* (the protective spirit of a place—such as Athena embodying the wisdom and civic spirit of Athens). Crucially, Blake calls this guardian figure a **"mental deity."** The ancient poets knew that Athena or any other local god was not a literal tyrant sitting on a cloud in outer space, but a *mental* creation—a poetic personification of that specific community's shared imagination, values, and psychological character.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**  
  Have you ever noticed how every town or country has its own special feeling, favorite foods, music, and way of doing things? The ancient poets noticed that too! They studied what made each city and country special, and they created a symbol—like a mascot or a story character that lived inside people's minds (a **"mental deity"**)—to stand for the heart and spirit of that city.

---

### Sentence 3
> **Original Text:**  
> "Till a system was formed, which some took advantage of and enslaved the vulgar by attempting to realize or abstract the mental deities from their objects."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**  
  *Translation:* Eventually, however, these fluid poetic metaphors were organized into a rigid ideological system, which opportunistic manipulators exploited in order to subjugate ordinary, uncritical people ("the vulgar") by severing ("abstracting") the mental deities from the living objects they represented and claiming they were literal, external rulers ("attempting to realize" them).  
  *Implied Meaning:* This is the pivotal philosophical sentence of the passage. How does liberating poetry degenerate into oppressive dogma? Through two twin errors of Reason: **abstraction** and **literalization ("realizing")**.  
  1. First, opportunists **abstracted** (pulled away) the poetic spirit from the living river, forest, or human community it belonged to, draining the natural world of its sacredness.  
  2. Second, they **realized** (treated as a literal, external object) those metaphors, claiming that the gods were invisible, authoritarian monarchs living apart from humanity in the sky. Once people believed that power belonged to an external, invisible ruler—and that only a select caste of intermediaries could speak to that ruler—ordinary people ("the vulgar") were mentally and politically enslaved.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**  
  Everything was wonderful as long as everyone remembered that those story characters were poetic symbols for the beauty of nature and human hearts. However, then some sneaky, bossy people saw a chance to take over! They took all those beautiful poems and turned them into a strict **system** of rules. They pulled the story spirits away from the real trees, rivers, and people, and told everyone: *"Those spirits aren't inside nature or inside your imagination anymore—they are invisible bosses floating far away in the sky, and you have to obey us because we work for them!"* By tricking everyday people with this story, the bossy people took control over them.

---

### Sentence 4
> **Original Text:**  
> "Thus began Priesthood."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**  
  *Translation:* In this exact manner, institutional Priesthood came into existence.  
  *Implied Meaning:* With devastating brevity, Blake defines "Priesthood" (institutionalized religious authority) not as a divinely ordained office, but as a parasitic caste born from the theft and literalization of poetry. Just as the "villain" in *The Argument* waited until the "just man" had planted roses and springs before stealing the path, the Priest waits until the Poet has created inspiring visions and then turns those visions into instruments of social control.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**  
  In just three short words, Blake says: **That is how bossy rule-makers ("Priesthood") got started!** They didn't invent anything themselves—they just took the wonderful stories that poets made up and turned them into a bossy club where they got to be in charge.

---

### Sentence 5
> **Original Text:**  
> "Choosing forms of worship from poetic tales."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**  
  *Translation:* These priests constructed mandatory religious rituals, ceremonies, and dogmas ("forms of worship") by taking symbolic episodes out of ancient imaginative stories ("poetic tales") and treating them as literal requirements.  
  *Implied Meaning:* Rituals and dogmas are fossilized metaphors. What a poet originally intended as a vivid symbolic image of inner spiritual life—a "poetic tale"—the priest extracts and turns into an obligatory outward ceremony ("forms of worship") that everyone must mechanically repeat.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**  
  Where did these rule-makers get their strict rules and ceremonies? They literally opened up the storybooks written by the ancient poets ("poetic tales"), picked out parts of the stories, and turned them into strict chores and ceremonies that everyone was forced to follow!

---

### Sentence 6
> **Original Text:**  
> "And at length they pronounced that the Gods had ordered such things."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**  
  *Translation:* Finally, to cement their authority beyond question, the priests declared that the external Gods themselves had commanded these rituals and laws.  
  *Implied Meaning:* To prevent anyone from questioning their man-made system, institutional authorities erase the human, poetic origin of their rules and claim divine sanction ("the Gods had ordered such things"). Once a human invention is disguised as an unchangeable divine decree, questioning the priest is branded as rebellion against God.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**  
  To make sure nobody ever argued with them, the bossy rule-makers announced: *"We didn't make up these rules—the invisible Gods in the sky ordered you to do everything we say!"* That way, people were too scared to ask questions.

---

### Sentence 7
> **Original Text:**  
> "Thus men forgot that all deities reside in the human breast."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**  
  *Translation:* As a result of this long historical deception, humanity lost sight of the fundamental spiritual truth: that every divine power, god, and creative spirit actually dwells within the human heart and imagination ("in the human breast").  
  *Implied Meaning:* This concluding sentence is one of the most profound and celebrated declarations in all of Blake’s writings. It is not an expression of cold atheism (which denies the spiritual altogether), but of **radical spiritual humanism**. The qualities we worship as "divine"—boundless love, wisdom, mercy, righteous wrath, and creative power—are not alienated from us in a distant sky or locked inside dead nature; they live inside the **Human Imagination** ("the human breast"). When human beings project their own sacred creative powers onto external idols or authoritarian systems, they alienate themselves from their own divinity. True liberation occurs when we wake up and reclaim the divine spark within our own hearts.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**  
  Because of that trick, people forgot the biggest, most wonderful secret in the universe: **All the goodness, wisdom, love, and creative magic that people were looking for far away in the sky actually live right inside our own human hearts ("in the human breast")!** Whenever you use your imagination, show deep kindness, or create something wonderful, that sacred spark is shining right inside of you!

## Comprehensive Summaries of Section 5: "The Ancient Poets"

### 1. Detailed Summary for Adults and Educators
In **Section 5 (Plate 11)**, William Blake provides a concise genealogy of how human imagination gives birth to culture and how institutional authority subsequently corrupts that imagination into dogma:
* **The Poetic Origin of Perception:** Originally, "ancient poets" possessed expanded sensory and imaginative faculties ("enlarged and numerous senses"). Rather than viewing nature and society as dead mechanisms, they perceived the living spiritual identity ("Genius" or "mental deity") animating every forest, river, mountain, and city. Crucially, these deities were understood as *mental* and poetic realities uniting humanity with the living world.
* **The Fall into Abstraction and Priesthood:** The corruption of consciousness occurred when calculating systematizers **abstracted** (separated) these "mental deities" from the living world and **realized** (literalized) them into external, authoritarian rulers. By turning fluid "poetic tales" into rigid "forms of worship" and claiming that external Gods had commanded them, "Priesthood" enslaved the populace through fear and dependency.
* **Reclaiming the Inner Divine:** The tragedy of this alienation is that *"men forgot that all deities reside in the human breast."* For Blake, the divine is not an external monarch demanding passive submission, but the infinite creative Imagination dwelling within humanity itself.

### 2. Detailed Summary for an 8-Year-Old Learner
In **Section 5: The Ancient Poets**, William Blake tells the true story of how poems and stories got turned into bossy rulebooks—and how we can remember the magic inside our own hearts:
1. **How Poets Saw the World:** Long ago, the first poets looked at woods, rivers, mountains, and cities with big imaginations. They gave names and personalities ("Geniuses" or "mental deities") to every place to celebrate how alive and special the world was.
2. **How Bossy Rule-Makers Took Over:** Later on, some greedy people took those imaginative poems and turned them into a strict **system**. They told everyone that the spirits in the poems were actually invisible bosses far away in the sky who ordered everyone to obey the rule-makers ("Priesthood").
3. **The Big Secret Inside Your Heart:** Because of that trick, people forgot that **all the wonderful power of love, wisdom, and creativity actually lives right inside the human heart ("in the human breast")!** Blake wants us to wake up and remember that the creative spark is inside us, not locked away by bossy rule-makers.
