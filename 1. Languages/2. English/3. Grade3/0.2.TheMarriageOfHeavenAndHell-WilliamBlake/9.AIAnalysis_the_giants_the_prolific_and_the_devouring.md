# Comprehensive Study Guide: Section 9 – The Giants, the Prolific, and the Devouring

**From William Blake’s *The Marriage of Heaven and Hell* (Plates 16–17)**

*(Note on Section 9: Immediately following the "Printing-House in Hell"—where living molten metals from the furnace of imagination were cooled into books and "arranged in libraries" by ordinary men—Blake presents one of the most profound philosophical passages in the entire work. Expanding upon his opening doctrine from Plate 3 ["Without contraries is no progression"], Blake now defines the two eternal classes of human beings and cosmic forces: **the Prolific** [the creators and producers of life, art, and energy] and **the Devouring** [the consumers, organizers, and rationalizers who receive and categorize that energy].)*

---

## Sentence-by-Sentence Breakdown

### Sentence 1

> **Original Text:**
> "The Giants who formed this world into its sensual existence and now seem to live in it in chains are in truth the causes of its life and the sources of all activity, but the chains are the cunning of weak and tame minds, which have power to resist energy, according to the proverb, “The weak in courage is strong in cunning.”"

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* The primordial, titanic creative forces ("The Giants") that originally shaped this world into a vibrant, sensory reality—and who today appear to sit bound in chains beneath society's rules—are in reality the very engines of the world’s life and the true origin of all human action; meanwhile, the chains that seem to bind them are merely the manipulative schemes ("cunning") of timid, domesticated minds that possess the negative power to resist and restrain creative energy, just as the Proverb of Hell stated: *"The weak in courage is strong in cunning."*
  *Implied Meaning:* Blake draws simultaneously on Greek mythology (the **Titans** or **Giants** overthrown and chained in Tartarus by the calculating sky-god Zeus) and biblical mythology (the **Nephilim** or "giants in the earth" before the Flood in Genesis 6:4). In Blake’s psychological reading of myth, these "Giants" represent humanity’s original, unfallen **Energies, passions, and imaginative powers** that formed the living, sensory world ("its sensual existence"). 
  Look at the visual illustration Blake etched at the top of Plate 16: five giant figures sit huddled in a stone dungeon, looking bound. Yet Blake reveals a paradox: even though these Giants *seem* to live in chains under the rule of polite society, they are actually the sole source of all life and activity in the world! The "chains" are not stronger than the Giants; rather, they are the legalistic, moralistic, and psychological tricks ("cunning") used by cowardly, conformist minds ("weak and tame minds") to make creative people feel guilty or restrained—quoting his own Proverb 49 from the *Proverbs of Hell*.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  In ancient stories and myths, there are tales about mighty **Giants** who built the mountains, rivers, and the whole wide world we can see, hear, and touch ("its sensual existence"), and who later got tied up in heavy chains. 
  Blake tells us a secret about who those Giants really are: **The Giants are our big Creative Energies, our passions, and our imaginations!** Even though it sometimes looks like strict rules have locked those Giants up in chains, those Giants are still the real power that makes the whole world move, grow, and stay alive! 
  Then where did the chains come from? They were made by timid, scared minds ("weak and tame minds") who were afraid of big energy. Remember Proverb 49 from earlier—*"The weak in courage is strong in cunning"*? Because timid people aren't brave enough to be Giants themselves, they use sneaky rules and guilt trips ("cunning") like invisible chains to try to hold big Energy back!

### Sentence 2

> **Original Text:**
> "Thus one portion of being is the Prolific, the other the Devouring."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* Consequently, all of human existence is divided into two fundamental, interacting modes of being: **the Prolific** (that which creates, produces, and overflows with new life) and **the Devouring** (that which consumes, absorbs, analyzes, and contains what has been produced).
  *Implied Meaning:* Here Blake elevates his earlier contrast between **Energy** and **Reason** (or the **Fountain** and the **Cistern**) into two universal ontological categories:
  * **The Prolific:** From the Latin *proles* (offspring) + *facere* (to make). The Prolific is the artist, the poet, the inventor, the prophet, and the life-giving imagination that continually brings forth new ideas, works, and delights.
  * **The Devouring:** The Devourer is the rationalist, the critic, the classifier, the administrator, and the consumer who cannot generate original life on their own, and therefore subsists by taking in, organizing, and consuming the creations of the Prolific.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Blake says that all living things and all human minds work in two big ways, like two halves of a puzzle:
  1. **The Prolific (The Makers and Growers):** "Prolific" means full of life and always making new things—like an apple tree growing hundreds of apples, a fountain bubbling with fresh water, or an artist painting brand-new pictures!
  2. **The Devouring (The Eaters and Organizers):** "Devouring" means taking in and using up what the Makers made—like people picking and eating the apples, or librarians putting finished books onto neat shelves!

### Sentence 3

> **Original Text:**
> "To the devourer it seems as if the producer was in his chains; but it is not so, he only takes portions of existence, and fancies that the whole."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* From the arrogant perspective of the Devourer (the rationalist/consumer), it looks as though the Producer (the creative genius) is completely subjugated under his control ("in his chains"); however, that is an illusion—the Devourer is only capable of grasping small, finite fragments ("portions") of the Producer’s infinite creation, and foolishly flatters himself that those fragments are the entirety of reality ("fancies that the whole").
  *Implied Meaning:* Think back to Section 2 (*The Voice of the Devil*), where Reason flattered itself that it had conquered Desire, and Section 8 (*The Printing-House in Hell*), where the men in the sixth chamber arranged the fiery creations of Hell into neat library books. 
  The rationalist critic, ruler, or scholar ("the devourer") looks at the rules, categories, and institutions he has built and thinks, *"I have tamed the Giants! Creative energy is my servant!"* Blake exposes this as a delusion of grandeur: the Prolific imagination is **infinite**, whereas the Devourer’s rational mind is bounded by the five senses. The Devourer can only bite off and digest tiny "portions of existence" at a time, mistaking his little bucket of water for the entire ocean!

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Sometimes the **Devourer** (the bossy organizer or rule-maker) looks at the **Producer** (the creative Giant) and brags, *"Aha! I put my rules around you, so I am the boss of you and have you in my chains!"*
  Blake laughs and says, *"That isn't true at all!"* Imagine someone dipping one little teacup into the giant ocean and saying, *"Look! I caught the whole ocean in my cup!"* The Devourer can only scoop up tiny pieces ("portions") of the Giant’s endless creativity, and he foolishly tricks himself into thinking his little teacup holds the whole universe!

---

### Sentence 4

> **Original Text:**
> "But the Prolific would cease to be prolific unless the Devourer as a sea received the excess of his delights."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* Nevertheless, the creative Producer ("the Prolific") would no longer be able to keep generating new life unless the Consumer ("the Devourer"), acting like a vast ocean basin, continually received and absorbed the overflowing abundance ("the excess") of the Producer’s creative joys.
  *Implied Meaning:* This is a breathtaking dialectical twist that proves Blake is **not** advocating the destruction of Reason or the Devourer! Just as *"Without contraries is no progression"* (Plate 3) and *"Reason is the bound or outward circumference of Energy"* (Plate 4), the Prolific actually **needs** the Devourer! 
  Why? Imagine a river that endlessly overflows with water ("the excess of his delights"), but has no sea or ocean basin to flow into: the river would back up, flood its own source, and stagnate! Similarly, an artist, poet, or creator ("the Prolific") needs an audience, a reader, a society, and an organizing structure ("the Devourer as a sea") to receive, absorb, and use the overflowing abundance of their creations so that the creator is freed to create anew.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Now Blake surprises us with an amazing twist! Since the **Prolific** (the Maker) is so creative and the **Devourer** (the Receiver/Eater) just takes what the Maker makes, does that mean we should get rid of the Devourer?
  **No!** Blake says that the Maker actually *needs* the Receiver! 
  Think about a rushing river full of fresh, sparkling water (the **Prolific**). Where does all that water go? It flows into the giant **Sea** (the **Devourer**)! What would happen if the Sea wasn't there to catch all that extra water? The river would get backed up and have nowhere to flow! Or think about an apple tree: if nobody ever picked and ate its apples, the ground would get too crowded for new seeds to grow! The Maker needs someone to receive and enjoy all the wonderful things it creates ("the excess of his delights") so it can keep making more!

### Sentence 5

> **Original Text:**
> "Some will say, “Is not God alone the Prolific?”"

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* Orthodox theologians and pious moralists will object: "Isn't God in Heaven the sole Creator ('the Prolific'), while all human beings are merely passive creatures?"
  *Implied Meaning:* Blake anticipates the standard objection of traditional dualistic religion. Orthodox theology taught that God alone is the Creator (*ex nihilo*), located outside the world, and that for human beings to claim divine creative power ("the Prolific") is the sin of pride or blasphemy.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Blake knows that some strict religious teachers will raise their hands and ask: *"Wait a minute! Isn't God in the sky the ONLY real Maker ('the Prolific'), and aren't human beings just supposed to sit quietly and not call themselves Creators?"*

---

### Sentence 6

> **Original Text:**
> "I answer: “God only acts and is in existing beings or men.”"

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* To that objection I reply: "God has no abstract, separate existence apart from living creation; God only acts, creates, and has His being *within* and *through* living beings and human minds!"
  *Implied Meaning:* Pairing directly with his famous declaration at the end of Section 5 (*"Thus men forgot that all deities reside in the human breast"*), this sentence is the cornerstone of Blake’s **immanent theology**. God is not a distant monarch or cosmic watchmaker sitting in empty space outside the universe. Rather, divine creativity ("the Prolific") lives, breathes, and acts *only* through living beings and human imagination ("in existing beings or men"). Therefore, whenever a human being creates art, speaks truth, loves generously, or exercises the Poetic Genius, *that is God acting in the world!*

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Blake gives a powerful, clear answer: **"God only acts and is in existing beings or men."**
  That means God isn't a faraway boss sitting alone on a cloud doing everything by Himself while we just watch! Instead, God’s creative power lives and works **right inside living creatures and human beings!** Whenever you use your imagination to create something good, kind, and wonderful, God’s creative spark is working right through *your* hands and *your* heart!

---

### Sentence 7

> **Original Text:**
> "These two classes of men are always upon earth, and they should be enemies: whoever tries to reconcile them seeks to destroy existence."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* These two fundamental types of human beings—the Prolific (visionary creators) and the Devouring (rationalist organizers)—are permanently present in human society, and they *ought* to remain in active, uncompromising opposition ("should be enemies"); anyone who attempts to erase their differences and force them into a lukewarm compromise ("reconcile them") is actually trying to destroy the dynamic tension that keeps human life alive!
  *Implied Meaning:* How can a book titled *The **Marriage** of Heaven and Hell* say that the Prolific and the Devouring **"should be enemies"** and that trying to **"reconcile them"** destroys existence? 
  This is a crucial distinction in Blake’s philosophy between **Creative Opposition (Marriage/Contraries)** and **Lukewarm Compromise (Reconciliation/Uniformity)**:
  * In a true Blakean "Marriage" of Contraries, each side remains 100% true to its own distinct nature, pushing against the other in fierce, honest intellectual and artistic debate ("mental fight" or spiritual enmity). Just as the positive and negative poles of a battery must remain opposite in order for electricity to flow, the Prolific and the Devourer must remain vigorous opponents ("enemies" in the sense of distinct, unyielding contraries).
  * If you "reconcile" the positive and negative poles of a battery by blending them into a neutral gray middle, the spark dies and all energy stops! Whoever tries to make the wild artist and the cautious bureaucrat meet in the middle in polite, conformist agreement destroys the vital spark of human existence.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Wait a second—why does Blake say that these two types of people (the **Makers** and the **Organizers**) are always on Earth and **"should be enemies,"** and that trying to make them agree on everything (**"reconcile them"**) would ruin life?!
  Here, Blake does *not* mean being mean or hurting each other! Think of two teams playing an exciting game of soccer or tug-of-war, or think of the North Pole and South Pole of a magnet. In a game of soccer, the two teams are "opponents" ("enemies" on the field) pushing hard against each other. What would happen if someone walked onto the field and said, *"Stop pushing against each other! Let's just stand in the middle and do nothing so nobody disagrees!"*? The game would be completely ruined and nobody would ever move! 
  Blake is saying that **Big Dreamers** and **Careful Rule-Keepers** are supposed to challenge each other, debate, and push against each other—because that exciting tug-of-war is what keeps the world moving forward!

---

### Sentence 8

> **Original Text:**
> "Religion is an endeavour to reconcile the two."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* Institutionalized Religion is precisely this misguided attempt to force the Prolific and the Devourer into a false, uniform compromise—subjugating creative Energy under the guise of peaceful harmony.
  *Implied Meaning:* Why does Blake critique institutional "Religion" so fiercely throughout this book? Because orthodox Religion preaches a false peace: it tries to tame the fierce Lion of creative Energy so that it will lie down obediently inside the Cistern of the Devourer, demanding that everyone conform to a single moral code (recall Plate 24: *"One law for the lion and ox is Oppression"*). By trying to smooth over the vital clash between visionary Genius and conventional Reason, institutional religion deadens both.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Blake says that strict, bossy **Religion** is what happens when rule-makers try to stop that exciting tug-of-war and force everyone to act the exact same way—telling the brave Lions and Eagles that they have to act just like quiet, tame sheep!

---

### Sentence 9

> **Original Text:**
> "Note.—Jesus Christ did not wish to unite but to separate them, as in the parable of sheep and goats; and He says: “I came not to send peace, but a sword.”"

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* Note: Jesus Christ Himself never sought to blend these two states into a lukewarm compromise, but rather to distinguish and separate them sharply—as illustrated in His parable of the sheep and the goats (Matthew 25:31–33), and in His own declaration: *"I came not to send peace, but a sword"* (Matthew 10:34).
  *Implied Meaning:* Orthodox churches portrayed Jesus as a gentle teacher of polite social conformity who wanted everyone to be nice, docile, and agreeable. Blake quotes Jesus’s own words from the Gospel of Matthew to prove the opposite! 
  1. **The Parable of the Sheep and the Goats (Matthew 25:32):** Jesus separates the sheep from the goats rather than pretending they are the same animal (and remember that in Blake’s Proverb 23, *"The lust of the goat is the bounty of God"*—both have their distinct nature!).
  2. **"I came not to send peace, but a sword" (Matthew 10:34):** In the Gospel, Jesus declares that true spiritual awakening is not a sedative that puts people to sleep in false "peace"; it is a sharp **sword of truth** that cuts through hypocrisy, divides living truth from dead convention, and sparks fierce spiritual transformation.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Strict teachers often told people that Jesus just wanted everyone to sit quietly, never argue, and never rock the boat. However, Blake opens up the Bible and shows what **Jesus Christ** actually said! 
  In one story, Jesus separated the **sheep** and the **goats** into two distinct groups because sheep and goats are different animals with different natures. And Jesus also said, *"I came not to send peace, but a sword"*—which means he didn't come to put people's minds to sleep with boring, fake agreement; he came to wake people up with the sharp truth so they would think bravely!

### Sentence 10

> **Original Text:**
> "Messiah, or Satan, or Tempter, was formerly thought to be one of the antediluvians who are our Energies."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* The figure variously named the Messiah, Satan, or the Tempter was originally understood in ancient myth to be one of the titanic pre-Flood beings ("antediluvians")—who are, in reality, our own primordial human Energies!
  *Implied Meaning:* Blake concludes Section 9 by circling all the way back to **Sentence 1 ("The Giants")** and to **Section 2 (*The Voice of the Devil*, where Milton's Messiah and Job's Satan were compared)**! 
  The word **"antediluvians"** literally means "those who lived before the Biblical Flood (*deluge*)"—specifically the mighty **Giants** (*Nephilim*) of Genesis 6:4 and the ancient patriarchs who lived before the floodwaters of narrow five-senses materialism drowned the ancient world. 
  Whether orthodox tradition calls that titanic force "Satan" or "the Tempter" (because it tempts humanity to break out of narrow rules and eat from the Tree of Life) or whether prophetic tradition hails it as the liberating "Messiah," ancient myth recognized that these towering figures are actually **the giant creative Energies living inside the human psyche ("who are our Energies")**!

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Look at the big word **"antediluvians"**: *ante* means "before," and *diluvian* means "the Giant Flood" (from the story of Noah's Ark). In the old stories, right before the Giant Flood, the Earth was full of those mighty **Giants** we talked about in Sentence 1!
  Blake ties the whole chapter together by telling us that whether old stories called that giant power "the Messiah," "Satan," or "the Tempter" (the voice that dares you to try new things), people long ago knew that those pre-Flood **Giants** were really a symbol for **our own giant Creative Energies living inside us!**

## Comprehensive Summaries of Section 9: "The Giants, the Prolific, and the Devouring"

### 1. Detailed Summary for Adults and Educators

In **Section 9 (Plates 16–17)**, William Blake deepens his doctrine of Contraries into a comprehensive theory of culture, psychology, and ontology centered on **the Prolific** and **the Devouring**:

* **The Chained Giants of Energy:** Alluding to the Titans of Greek myth and the pre-Flood Giants (*Nephilim*) of Genesis, Blake identifies the "Giants who formed this world into its sensual existence" as humanity’s own primal, creative **Energies**. Although they appear to sit in chains beneath rationalist society, they remain the sole source of all life and activity; their chains are merely the psychological "cunning of weak and tame minds" that resist energy out of cowardice.
* **The Dialectical Necessity of the Prolific and the Devouring:** Existence is divided into **the Prolific** (the creative producer/artist) and **the Devouring** (the rational consumer/organizer). While the Devourer arrogantly imagines that he holds the Producer in chains, he actually grasps only tiny, finite "portions of existence." Yet the relationship is mutually necessary: *"the Prolific would cease to be prolific unless the Devourer as a sea received the excess of his delights."* Furthermore, God is not an external creator separate from humanity, for *"God only acts and is in existing beings or men."*
* **Against False Reconciliation:** Because progression requires the sharp friction of opposites, the Prolific and the Devourer **"should be enemies"** (engaged in vigorous mental opposition). Institutional **Religion** tries to "reconcile the two" into a lukewarm, conformist sameness, which seeks to destroy existence. By contrast, **Jesus Christ** sought to keep the distinction sharp—separating sheep from goats and bringing the "sword" of awakening truth rather than the stupor of false peace.

---

### 2. Detailed Summary for an 8-Year-Old Learner

In **Section 9: The Giants, the Prolific, and the Devouring**, William Blake explains who the real "Giants" of the world are and how two very different kinds of minds keep the world moving:

1. **You Have Giant Energy Inside You:** Old stories talk about mighty **Giants** (also called "antediluvians," from before the Great Flood) who built the world and then got tied up in chains. Blake says those Giants are really **our own big Creative Energies**! Timid, scared people use sneaky rules ("cunning") like chains to try to hold big Energy back, even though Energy is what keeps the whole world alive!
2. **The Makers ("The Prolific") and the Receivers ("The Devouring"):** There are two kinds of powers in the world: **The Prolific** (the Makers, who overflow with new ideas like a rushing river) and **The Devouring** (the Organizers and Receivers, who take in those ideas like the giant sea). Even though the Organizers sometimes brag that they are the bosses, they can only scoop up tiny pieces of what the Makers create. At the same time, just like a river needs an ocean to flow into, the Makers need the Receivers to enjoy all the wonderful things they make!
3. **Why Opposites Shouldn't Be Blended into Sameness:** Blake reminds us that God’s creative power works *right inside human beings* ("in existing beings or men"). And just like two teams in a game of tug-of-war or soccer, the **Big Dreamers** and the **Rule-Keepers** are supposed to challenge each other instead of being forced by bossy Religion to act the exact same way! Even Jesus said he came to wake people up with truth rather than put everyone to sleep with fake agreement.
