# Comprehensive Study Guide: Section 12 – A Memorable Fancy (The Devil in a Flame of Fire and the Conversion of the Angel)

**From William Blake’s *The Marriage of Heaven and Hell* (Plates 22–24)**

*(Note on Section 12: This fifth and final "Memorable Fancy" serves as the dramatic climax of the prose sections of *The Marriage of Heaven and Hell*. In Section 10, the Angel stubbornly refused to alter his opinion and fled back into his dry "Analytics." Now, however, a "Devil in a flame of fire" confronts an "Angel that sat on a cloud" in a direct theological debate over the nature of God, humanity, and Jesus Christ. By demonstrating that Jesus himself lived by spontaneous love and creative "impulse, not from rules," the Devil finally convinces the Angel to step out of his cloud, embrace the flame of creative Energy, and transform into the fiery prophet Elijah—achieving the actual **"Marriage" of Heaven and Hell**!)*

---

## Sentence-by-Sentence Breakdown

### Sentence 1

> **Original Text:**
> "Once I saw a Devil in a flame of fire, who arose before an Angel that sat on a cloud, and the Devil uttered these words: “The worship of God is, honouring His gifts in other men each according to his genius, and loving the greatest men best."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* On one occasion I beheld a spirit of creative Energy ("a Devil") rising up within a blazing pillar of fire in front of an orthodox moralist ("an Angel") who was perched comfortably upon a murky cloud, and the Devil proclaimed: "True worship of the Divine consists in honoring and celebrating the sacred talents ('gifts') present within our fellow human beings—each according to their unique creative nature ('genius')—and loving the most profoundly inspired and magnanimous human beings most of all."
  *Implied Meaning:* Look first at the contrasting visual stage:
  * **The Devil in a Flame of Fire:** Rises upward from below like living, self-luminous energy (echoing Jehovah in the Burning Bush or the pillar of fire in Exodus).
  * **The Angel Sitting on a Cloud:** Sits passively above on an opaque vapor ("a cloud"—Blake’s symbol for murky, obscured rationalist abstraction and aloof moral superiority).
  The Devil immediately redefines **"The worship of God."** Because *"all deities reside in the human breast"* (Plate 11) and *"God only acts and is in existing beings or men"* (Plate 16), you cannot worship God by bowing down to an invisible abstraction in the sky while despising humanity! True worship is **humanistic and celebratory**: recognizing, honoring, and nurturing the unique spark of Poetic Genius in every person (echoing Proverb 17: *"The most sublime act is to set another before you"*).

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Blake sees an amazing scene! Sitting high up on a misty, foggy cloud is a strict **Angel** (who represents bossy rules). Rising up right in front of him inside a bright, warm **flame of fire** is a **Devil** (who represents Creative Energy and Truth).
  The fiery spirit speaks up and shares a wonderful secret about what it really means to honor and love God: **"The real way to worship God is to honor and celebrate the special gifts and talents inside other people—cheering for each person's unique imagination ('his genius'), and loving the kindest, wisest, and most inspiring people most of all!"**

### Sentence 2

> **Original Text:**
> "Those who envy or calumniate great men hate God, for there is no other God.”"

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* "Conversely, those petty minds who harbor jealousy toward ('envy') or slander and speak maliciously against ('calumniate') great artists, prophets, and visionaries actually hate God Himself—for God exists nowhere else except within living human greatness!"
  *Implied Meaning:* Throughout history, mediocre conformists and orthodox authorities have attacked visionary geniuses (such as Socrates, Jesus, Dante, Milton, or Blake himself), slandering ("calumniating") their passion as heresy, pride, or madness. Blake’s Devil exposes the hypocrisy of pious critics who claim to love God in church while tearing down inspired human beings in daily life: since the Divine is manifest only within the living Poetic Genius of humanity (*"for there is no other God"*), slandering human greatness out of small-minded envy is direct hatred of the Divine.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  The word **"calumniate"** means telling mean lies or gossiping about someone to make them look bad because you are jealous ("envy") of how talented or brave they are.
  The fiery spirit explains that when grumpy people get jealous of great artists, thinkers, and helpers and try to tear them down, they are actually hating God's own light—because God's goodness and creativity shine right inside human hearts (*"for there is no other God"*)!

### Sentence 3

> **Original Text:**
> "The Angel hearing this became almost blue, but mastering himself he grew yellow, and at last white-pink and smiling, and then replied: “Thou idolater, is not God One? and is not He visible in Jesus Christ? and has not Jesus Christ given His sanction to the law of ten commandments? and are not all other men fools, sinners, and nothings?”"

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* Upon hearing this radical humanist declaration, the Angel flushed dark blue with livid outrage; however, forcing his anger under polite control ("mastering himself"), he turned bilious yellow with jealousy, and finally settled into a pale, rosy-white complexion with a hypocritical smile, replying: "You blasphemous worshiper of human idols ('Thou idolater')! Is there not only One God? And is He not revealed exclusively in Jesus Christ? And did not Jesus Christ officially endorse and enforce the legal code of the Ten Commandments? And are not all other human beings on earth mere fools, depraved sinners, and worthless nobodies ('nothings')?"
  *Implied Meaning:* This is one of Blake’s greatest psychological portraits of **repressed hypocrisy**:
  1. **The Color-Shift of Repression (Blue $\rightarrow$ Yellow $\rightarrow$ White-Pink and Smiling):** When the Angel hears that God lives in human genius, his immediate reaction is furious rage (**"almost blue"**—like a livid bruise or choking fury). Instead of expressing honest wrath like a lion, the Angel represses his anger (**"mastering himself"**), which turns into jaundiced envy and spite (**"grew yellow"**—echoing Proverb 5: *"He who desires, but acts not, breeds pestilence"*). Finally, he puts on the polite mask of pious respectability (**"white-pink and smiling"**—recall Proverb 32’s *"The selfish smiling fool"* and *The Argument*’s *"sneaking serpent"* walking in *"mild humility"*!).
  2. **The Orthodox Catechism of Human Worthlessness:** Behind his polite smile, the Angel’s theology is profoundly misanthropic. He uses the exclusivity of God and a legalistic view of Jesus (as the enforcer of the Ten Commandments) to crush human self-worth, declaring that all human beings are **"fools, sinners, and nothings"** who must grovel before authority.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Watch what happens to the Angel's face when he hears that all people have a sacred gift inside them—it is like watching a mood ring change colors!
  * First, he gets so mad that his face turns **dark blue**!
  * Next, because Angels always pretend they never get angry, he swallows his anger down ("mastering himself"), which makes him turn **sour yellow** with jealousy!
  * Finally, he puts on a fake, polite **pink-and-white smile** on the outside so he looks nice while he says something super mean!
  With that fake smile, the Angel scolds the Devil: *"You are breaking the rules ('idolater')! Isn't there only One God, shown only in Jesus Christ? Didn't Jesus tell everyone they have to obey the strict rulebook of the Ten Commandments? And aren't **all other human beings just silly fools, bad sinners, and total nobodies ('nothings')**?"*

### Sentence 4

> **Original Text:**
> "The Devil answered: “Bray a fool in a mortar with wheat, yet shall not his folly be beaten out of him."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* The Devil replied by quoting the biblical Book of Proverbs against the Angel: "Even if you were to pound a stubborn fool with a pestle inside a stone mortar alongside grains of wheat, you still could not beat his foolishness out of him!"
  *Implied Meaning:* With razor-sharp wit, the Devil opens his rebuttal by quoting **Proverbs 27:22** verbatim from the King James Bible (*"Though thou shouldest bray a fool in a mortar among wheat with a pestle, yet will not his foolishness depart from him"*). The word **"bray"** means to pound, crush, or grind into powder in a mortar (just as husks are beaten off wheat). The Devil turns the Angel’s own word (*"fools"*) right back on him: the real "fool" is the dogmatic moralist whose mind is so hardened by self-righteous rules that no amount of argument can separate him from his folly!

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  The Devil answers the Angel by quoting a famous saying right out of the Bible’s Book of Proverbs!
  In olden days, bakers put wheat into a heavy stone bowl called a **mortar** and pounded it ("brayed" it) with a heavy club so the rough outer shells would pop right off the good wheat. The Devil jokes: *"Even if you put a stubborn, rule-obsessed fool into a stone bowl and pounded him alongside the wheat, his foolishness is stuck so tight it still wouldn't come off!"*

### Sentence 5

> **Original Text:**
> "If Jesus Christ is the greatest man, you ought to love Him in the greatest degree."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* "If, as you claim, Jesus Christ is the supreme embodiment of humanity ('the greatest man'), then by my very own principle—that we worship God by loving the greatest men best—you ought to love Him to the highest possible degree!"
  *Implied Meaning:* Instead of rejecting Jesus Christ, the Devil seamlessly shows that his opening statement in Sentence 1 (*"loving the greatest men best"*) completely embraces Jesus! For Blake, Jesus is not an aloof tyrant who makes all other humans "nothings"; rather, Jesus is **"the greatest man"** because the Poetic Genius, love, and divine imagination shone in him without limit.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Remember how in Sentence 1 the Devil said we should *"love the greatest people best"*? So the Devil tells the Angel: *"See? If **Jesus Christ** is the greatest, most loving person who ever lived, then my rule says you should love Him the very most of all!"*

### Sentence 6

> **Original Text:**
> "Now hear how He has given His sanction to the law of ten commandments."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* "Now listen closely to the actual historical record of the Gospels, and see how Jesus supposedly 'endorsed' ('given His sanction to') your rigid law of the Ten Commandments!"
  *Implied Meaning:* With biting irony, the Devil prepares to dismantle the Angel’s claim that Jesus was a conventional law-and-order moralist who came to enforce the stony rules of Mount Sinai.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Then the Devil says: *"You just claimed that Jesus only cared about following the strict rulebook of the Ten Commandments. Well, let's actually read the stories about Jesus in the Bible and see how He treated strict rules!"*

### Sentence 7

> **Original Text:**
> "Did He not mock at the Sabbath, and so mock the Sabbath’s God? murder those who were murdered because of Him? turn away the law from the woman taken in adultery, steal the labour of others to support Him? bear false witness when He omitted making a defence before Pilate? covet when He prayed for His disciples, and when He bid them shake off the dust of their feet against such as refused to lodge them?"

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* "Consider the Gospel accounts:
  1. *(Commandments 1–4: The Sabbath and its Lawgiver)* Did Jesus not openly defy the strict Sabbath rules—healing the sick and letting his disciples pluck grain on the Sabbath—and thereby defy the legalistic God of the Sabbath?
  2. *(Commandment 6: Murder)* Was He not, according to strict legal accountability, responsible for the deaths of those who were martyred for His cause (such as the Holy Innocents of Bethlehem, John the Baptist, or His disciples)?
  3. *(Commandment 7: Adultery)* Did He not block the Mosaic Law from being enforced when He rescued the woman caught in adultery from being stoned to death (John 8:3–11)?
  4. *(Commandment 8: Stealing)* Did He not live without earning wages or owning property, relying on the labor and harvest of others (such as plucking grain from farmers' fields or accepting support from followers) to sustain His ministry?
  5. *(Commandment 9: False Witness)* Did He not refuse to testify on His own behalf when questioned by the Roman governor Pontius Pilate, violating the legal duty to give a full defense in court (Matthew 27:12–14)?
  6. *(Commandment 10: Coveting)* And did He not 'desire' ('covet') what belonged to the world when He prayed for His disciples (John 17) and instructed them to expect food and lodging from towns—and to shake the dust off their feet in protest against any house that refused to take them in (Matthew 10:11–14)?"
  *Implied Meaning:* In a tour de force of subversive biblical knowledge, the Devil marches straight through the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20:1–17) to show that from the narrow perspective of a **strict, literalistic Pharisee or "Angel,"** Jesus broke or subverted almost every single one of them!
  Why did Jesus heal on the Sabbath, save the condemned woman from the stone-throwers, live freely off shared abundance without private property, and stand silent before Roman law? Because whenever a cold, rigid rule ("the Law") conflicted with **human compassion, forgiveness, healing, and spiritual freedom**, Jesus chose **compassion and freedom** over the rulebook!

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  The Devil lists real stories from the life of Jesus in the Bible to show that Jesus cared about **kindness, healing, and love**, even when grumpy rule-keepers complained that He wasn't following their rulebook!
  * **The Sabbath Rule:** When someone was sick or hungry on the day of rest (the Sabbath), the strict rule-keepers said it was against the rules to help them—but Jesus healed people and fed His friends anyway!
  * **Forgiveness Instead of Punishment:** When an angry crowd wanted to punish a woman who had made a mistake, Jesus stepped in front of them, stopped their mean law, and forgave her!
  * **Sharing Instead of Owning:** Jesus and His friends didn't use money or lock up food—they picked grain from the fields when they were hungry and shared whatever people gave them!
  * **Refusing to Argue in Court:** When the Roman ruler Pilate put Jesus on trial and demanded that He follow the court rules and argue for Himself, Jesus stayed completely silent!
  * **Wishing Big Things for His Friends:** And Jesus wished ("coveted") for His friends to be welcomed into people's homes and taken care of wherever they traveled!

### Sentence 8

> **Original Text:**
> "I tell you, no virtue can exist without breaking these ten commandments."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* "I declare to you that genuine, living goodness ('virtue') cannot even exist without transcending and breaking free from the rigid, negative prohibitions of these Ten Commandments!"
  *Implied Meaning:* Why does the Devil make such a provocative statement? Notice that the Ten Commandments are framed almost entirely as **negative prohibitions** (*"Thou shalt not..."*). In Blake’s ethics, merely refraining from action out of fear of punishment is *not* virtue—it is dead passivity (recall Proverb 5: *"He who desires, but acts not, breeds pestilence"*). True **"virtue"** (from the Latin *virtus*, meaning active strength, courage, and excellence) is positive, creative love in action. Whenever you act with bold, forgiving generosity, you inevitably break through the narrow, fearful boundaries that legalism sets up to keep people apart.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Why does the Devil say Something so surprising—that real goodness (**"virtue"**) has to break out of strict rulebooks?
  Because a strict rulebook mostly just says, *"Don't do this! Don't touch that! Sit still!"* However, sitting still like a statue isn't real goodness! Real goodness (**virtue**) means **doing brave, kind, helpful things**—like when Jesus healed a sick person on the day when the rulebook said *"Don't do any work!"* Sometimes doing the right, loving thing means stepping right past a grumpy, unfair rule!

### Sentence 9

> **Original Text:**
> "Jesus was all virtue, and acted from impulse, not from rules.”"

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* "Jesus Christ was the pure embodiment of living goodness ('all virtue'), and He acted spontaneously from the immediate, overflowing inspiration of love and imagination within His heart ('from impulse'), never from mechanical obedience to external rulebooks ('not from rules')."
  *Implied Meaning:* This crisp, nine-word sentence is the ethical pinnacle of *The Marriage of Heaven and Hell*.
  * **Acting from Rules (The Angel's Way):** A person who only helps others or behaves well because they consulted an external checklist of laws ("rules") and feared punishment is acting mechanically, without love or imagination.
  * **Acting from Impulse (Jesus's Way):** In Blake’s vocabulary, **"impulse"** does not mean careless selfishness; it means the immediate, uncalculated, spontaneous outpouring of the divine Spirit (Energy, compassion, and Poetic Genius) from within the human heart. Because Jesus was completely united with the divine Spirit inside him, every spontaneous impulse of his heart was pure love, healing, and truth ("all virtue").

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  This short sentence is one of the most important lessons in the whole book: **"Jesus was all virtue, and acted from impulse, not from rules."**
  What is the difference between acting from **rules** and acting from **impulse**?
  * **Acting from Rules:** Imagine someone falls down on the playground, and before you help them up, you pull out a giant rulebook, flip to page 42, and say, *"Let's see... Rule 42 says I have to help you, or else I'll get in trouble."* That isn't real love!
  * **Acting from Impulse:** Now imagine someone falls down, and right away—without checking a rulebook or worrying about getting a reward—your heart feels a warm spark of caring (**"impulse"**) and you run over to help them up!
  Blake is teaching us that **real goodness comes straight from a warm, loving heart ("impulse"), not from a cold checklist of rules!**

### Sentence 10

> **Original Text:**
> "When he had so spoken, I beheld the Angel, who stretched out his arms embracing the flame of fire, and he was consumed, and arose as Elijah."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* As soon as the Devil finished speaking these words, I watched in wonder as the Angel opened his arms wide and embraced the blazing flame of fire; his rigid angelic shell was completely burned away ("consumed"), and he rose up transformed into the fiery Old Testament prophet **Elijah**!
  *Implied Meaning:* This is the dramatic moment of the **Marriage of Heaven and Hell**!
  Remember Proverb 68: *"Truth can never be told so as to be understood and not to be believed."* In Section 10, the Angel was too frightened to listen and ran away into his mill. Here, however, hearing the undeniable truth about Jesus’s spontaneous love pierces right through the Angel’s dogmatic armor.
  Instead of shrinking back on his cloud, the Angel **stretches out his arms and embraces the flame of fire** (Reason embraces Energy; Heaven marries Hell!). Just as Section 7 promised that when creation is "consumed" in fire, it is not destroyed but appears "infinite and holy," the Angel is not killed by the fire—his fearful, self-righteous persona is burned away, and he rises as **Elijah** (the great Hebrew prophet associated with fire from heaven who ascended in a **chariot of fire** in 2 Kings 2:11, and who represents the awakened spirit of true prophecy)!

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Look at what happens right after the Devil finishes speaking!
  Instead of getting mad or running away like the Angel in the last chapter, this Angel realizes that the Devil is telling the absolute truth! So the Angel steps off his cold, foggy cloud, opens his arms wide in a giant hug (**"stretched out his arms embracing the flame of fire"**), and hugs the warm, bright fire of Creative Energy!
  Does the fire hurt the Angel? **No!** Just like a caterpillar turning into a butterfly, the fire melts away his grumpy, bossy shell, and he rises up as **Elijah**—the famous prophet from the Bible who rode across the sky in a glowing **chariot of fire**!
  This hug between the Angel and the Fire is the real **"Marriage of Heaven and Hell"** that the whole book is named after—when **Careful Thinking (the Angel)** and **Warm Creative Energy (the Fire)** hug each other and become one!

### Sentence 11

> **Original Text:**
> "Note.—This Angel, who is now become a Devil, is my particular friend; we often read the Bible together in its infernal or diabolical sense, which the world shall have if they behave well."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* Note: This former Angel, who has now transformed into a joyful spirit of creative Energy ("a Devil"), has become my close personal friend; the two of us frequently study the Bible together, reading it according to its energetic, visionary, and poetic meaning ("its infernal or diabolical sense")—an interpretation that I will share with the world if humanity shows itself ready and receptive ("if they behave well").
  *Implied Meaning:* Once Reason (the Angel) stops trying to tyrannize over Energy and instead embraces the fire of Imagination, Reason and Energy become intimate partners ("my particular friend"—recall Proverb 31: *"The bird a nest, the spider a web, man friendship"*).
  What do Blake and his newly awakened friend do together? They read the Bible **"in its infernal or diabolical sense"**!
  * To read the Bible in its **"angelic"** sense is to read it as a literal, repressive rulebook of "Thou shalt nots" and threats of punishment (which turned the Bible into a "deep pit" of chained monkeys in Section 10).
  * To read the Bible in its **"infernal or diabolical"** sense is to read it as a sublime work of the **Poetic Genius**—celebrating human energy, imagination, liberty, and spontaneous love!
  With a playful wink to the reader, Blake jokes that he will give the world this liberating reading of the Bible *"if they behave well"* (inverting the way parents and priests tell children to "behave well"!).

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Blake adds a fun note at the end of the story: **"This Angel, who is now become a Devil** [a member of Team Creative Energy!], **is my particular friend!"**
  Instead of fighting, Reason and Energy are now best friends! And what do they like to do together? They sit down and read the Bible together in its **"infernal sense"**—which means reading it not as a scary book of grumpy rules, but as an exciting book of poetry, big imagination, bravery, and love! Blake jokes with a twinkle in his eye that he will share that wonderful way of reading with the world *"if they behave well"* (which for Blake means if people keep their minds open and brave!).

### Sentence 12

> **Original Text:**
> "I have also the Bible of Hell, which the world shall have whether they will or no."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* Furthermore, I have composed my own original visionary scriptures—**"the Bible of Hell"**—which I am going to give to the world whether conventional society wants it or not!
  *Implied Meaning:* While the world may not yet be ready for a full reinterpretation of the traditional Bible ("if they behave well"), Blake announces that he will not wait for society’s polite permission to unleash his own prophetic works! **"The Bible of Hell"** refers both to *The Marriage of Heaven and Hell* itself (along with *A Song of Liberty* on the following plates) and to the grand cycle of illuminated prophetic books Blake was actively embarking upon in the 1790s (*America a Prophecy*, *Visions of the Daughters of Albion*, *The Book of Urizen*, etc.). Just as Isaiah said in Section 6, *"I cared not for consequences, but wrote,"* Blake declares that the world will receive his liberating vision *"whether they will or no."*

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Then Blake says something super bold: **"I also have the Bible of Hell** [his own brand-new books of fiery imagination and poetry!], **and I am going to share it with the world whether grumpy people like it or not!"**
  Just like the prophet Isaiah at dinner, a true artist doesn't wait around for bossy people to give him permission to share the truth!

### Sentence 13

> **Original Text:**
> "One law for the lion and ox is Oppression."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* Enforcing a single, uniform moral and legal code upon fundamentally different beings—such as the fierce, carnivorous lion and the patient, grass-eating ox—is the very definition of **Oppression**.
  *Implied Meaning:* Etched as a standalone maxim at the bottom of Plate 24 beneath a striking visual illustration of King Nebuchadnezzar crawling on all fours like a beast in the wilderness (from Daniel 4:33—showing what happens when a tyrant tries to rule by absolute law and is reduced to beastly madness), this seven-word proverb encapsulates Blake’s entire political, educational, and spiritual philosophy.
  Recall Proverb 50 (*"The apple tree never asks the beech how he shall grow, nor the lion the horse how he shall take his prey"*) and Proverb 8 (*"A fool sees not the same tree that a wise man sees"*). Every living creature and every human individual possesses a unique, irreducible inner nature (**genius**). The **Lion** (fiery, active, prophetic creative Energy) and the **Ox** (steady, patient, agricultural endurance) are both noble creatures, yet their natures and needs are completely different. When an authoritarian state, church, or school system imposes **"One law"** (standardized conformity) on everyone alike, it does not create justice—it creates **Oppression**!

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Blake ends this section with one of the most famous and wise sentences he ever wrote:
  > **"One law for the lion and ox is Oppression."**
  Think about a mighty, roaring **Lion** and a big, gentle farm **Ox**. They are both wonderful animals, but they are completely different! What if a bossy zookeeper made **One Single Rule** for every animal: *"Every animal must eat only dry grass and pull a heavy wooden cart all day"*? That rule might work for the Ox, but it would be terrible and unfair for the Lion! Or what if the rule was: *"Every animal must roar loudly and chase antelopes"*? That would be terrible for the Ox!
  **"Oppression"** means being unfair and crushing someone's spirit. Blake is teaching every teacher, parent, and student a golden lesson: **Every single person is unique!** Forcing everyone to think, learn, and act the exact same way under one rigid rule is unfair—true wisdom celebrates what makes each of us special!

## Comprehensive Summaries of Section 12: "A Memorable Fancy" (The Conversion of the Angel)

### 1. Detailed Summary for Adults and Educators

In **Section 12 (Plates 22–24)**, William Blake brings his series of *Memorable Fancies* to a triumphant philosophical and dramatic resolution:

* **The Humanist Definition of Worship:** Confronting an orthodox "Angel that sat on a cloud," a "Devil in a flame of fire" declares that true worship of God consists in honoring His gifts in other human beings according to their unique genius and loving the greatest individuals best—whereas envying or slandering great individuals is hatred of God, *"for there is no other God."*
* **The Unmasking of Legalism and the Radical Jesus:** When the Angel—shifting from blue rage to yellow jealousy to a hypocritical pink-and-white smile—claims that Jesus sanctioned the Ten Commandments and that all other humans are *"fools, sinners, and nothings,"* the Devil refutes him from the Gospels themselves. Demonstrating how Jesus repeatedly broke or transcended the literal commandments (healing on the Sabbath, saving the woman taken in adultery, living by shared abundance, and standing silent before Pilate), the Devil proclaims that **"Jesus was all virtue, and acted from impulse, not from rules."**
* **The Marriage of Heaven and Hell and Individual Liberty:** Convinced by the truth, the Angel stretches out his arms, embraces the flame of fire, is consumed, and arises transformed as the fiery prophet **Elijah**. Becoming the narrator’s close friend, this converted Angel now reads the Bible in its "infernal" (poetic and energetic) sense alongside Blake. Blake announces his own **"Bible of Hell"** and concludes the prose text with the immortal maxim of individual liberty: **"One law for the lion and ox is Oppression."**

### 2. Detailed Summary for an 8-Year-Old Learner

In **Section 12: A Memorable Fancy**, we finally get to see the real **"Marriage of Heaven and Hell"** happen right before our eyes:

1. **Celebrating the Gifts in Others:** A fiery **Devil** (Team Creative Energy) tells an **Angel** sitting on a cloud (Team Strict Rules) that the real way to honor God is to celebrate the special talents and imagination (**"genius"**) inside other people, instead of being jealous or calling people "nothings."
2. **Acting from a Loving Heart ("Impulse"), Not Just Rules:** When the Angel puts on a fake smile and says that Jesus only cared about the strict rulebook of the Ten Commandments, the Devil reminds him of all the times in the Bible when Jesus broke strict rules in order to heal sick people, forgive people, and share food! The Devil shares a wonderful truth: **"Jesus was all virtue, and acted from impulse, not from rules"**—meaning real goodness springs straight from a warm, caring heart rather than a cold checklist!
3. **The Big Hug in the Fire & The Lion and the Ox:** Hearing this truth, the Angel finally opens his arms, **hugs the warm flame of fire**, and transforms into the fiery prophet **Elijah**—becoming Blake’s best friend! Finally, Blake leaves us with a wise saying to remember forever: **"One law for the lion and ox is Oppression"**—because every person is unique, and forcing everyone to be the exact same is never fair!
