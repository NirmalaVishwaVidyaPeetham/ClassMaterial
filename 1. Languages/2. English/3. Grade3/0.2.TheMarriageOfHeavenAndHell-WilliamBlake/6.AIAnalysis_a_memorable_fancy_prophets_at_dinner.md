# Comprehensive Study Guide: Section 6 – A Memorable Fancy (Dining with the Prophets Isaiah and Ezekiel)

**From William Blake’s *The Marriage of Heaven and Hell* (Plates 12–13)**

*(Note on Section 6: Immediately following Section 5—where Blake explained how institutional "Priesthood" hijacked the visions of the ancient poets—he presents his second **"A Memorable Fancy."** Parodying Emanuel Swedenborg’s dry reports of conversing with spirits, Blake invites two of the greatest Hebrew prophets of the Old Testament, **Isaiah** and **Ezekiel**, to dinner. Below is the complete, sentence-by-sentence breakdown featuring the exact original text, its detailed adult philosophical meaning, and an explanation tailored for an 8-year-old learner.)*

## Sentence-by-Sentence Breakdown

### Sentence 1

> **Original Text:**
> "The Prophets Isaiah and Ezekiel dined with me, and I asked them how they dared so roundly to assert that God spoke to them, and whether they did not think at the time that they would be misunderstood, and so be the cause of imposition."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* The ancient biblical prophets Isaiah and Ezekiel joined me for dinner, and I asked them how they had the audacity to declare so boldly and unequivocally ("so roundly") that God had spoken to them—and whether they did not worry at the time that literal-minded people would misunderstand their poetic expressions and use them as an excuse to impose authoritarian dogma ("imposition") upon humanity.
  *Implied Meaning:* Blake opens with domestic humor, hosting two towering figures of biblical antiquity at his dinner table. Yet his question strikes at the core dilemma introduced in Section 5 (*The Ancient Poets*): throughout the Hebrew Bible, Isaiah and Ezekiel repeatedly preface their prophecies with *"Thus saith the Lord."* In Blake’s vocabulary, **"imposition"** signifies priestly fraud and dogmatic control over gullible minds (compare Proverb 42: *"He who has suffered you to impose on him knows you"*). Blake asks the prophets directly: *By using the metaphor that God spoke to you, didn't you fear that literal-minded priests would later twist your poetry into an authoritarian rulebook to enslave humanity?*

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Imagine sitting down at your kitchen table for dinner, and your two guests are **Isaiah** and **Ezekiel**—two famous wise writers (called **Prophets**) who lived thousands of years ago in Bible times!
  In their books, Isaiah and Ezekiel often wrote, *"God spoke to me!"* Remember how in the last chapter we learned that bossy rule-makers took ancient poems and used them to trick people? So over dinner, Blake asks Isaiah and Ezekiel a big question: *"How were you so brave to say out loud that God spoke to you? Weren't you worried that people wouldn't understand your poetry, and that bossy rule-makers would use your words to trick and boss people around ('be the cause of imposition')?"*

### Sentence 2

> **Original Text:**
> "Isaiah answered: “I saw no God, nor heard any, in a finite organical perception: but my senses discovered the infinite in everything; and as I was then persuaded, and remained confirmed, that the voice of honest indignation is the voice of God, I cared not for consequences, but wrote.”"

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* Isaiah replied: "I neither saw a physical figure of God nor heard an external sound through my limited bodily sense organs ('in a finite organical perception'); rather, my awakened senses uncovered the infinite spiritual reality within everything. And because I was deeply convinced then—and remain certain today—that the voice of righteous anger against injustice ('honest indignation') is the true voice of God, I paid no heed to worldly consequences, and simply wrote."
  *Implied Meaning:* This is one of the most important theological statements in Blake’s entire body of work. Isaiah dismantles both superstitious literalism and rationalist skepticism across three points:
  1. **Beyond "Finite Organical Perception":** The Divine is not a finite material object seen by the physical retina or heard by the physical eardrum ("organical" refers to the physical sense organs). Rather, prophetic vision occurs when human perception expands to **discover the infinite in everything** (just as the first *Memorable Fancy* saw an immense world of delight within a single flying bird).
  2. **Honest Indignation as the Divine Voice:** How did God speak to Isaiah? Through **"the voice of honest indignation"**—righteous anger against injustice, cruelty, and falsehood. When a human being witnesses oppression and feels a burning, unselfish passion for truth (like Rintrah’s roar in *The Argument*), that inner fire *is* the divine voice speaking within the human heart.
  3. **Fearless Expression:** A true visionary cannot censor the truth out of timid caution ("Prudence") merely because hypocrites might later misread or abuse it.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Isaiah gives an amazing answer! He explains: *"I didn't see God like a giant person standing in front of my eyeballs, and I didn't hear a loud booming sound in my ears ('in a finite organical perception'). Instead, when I looked at the world with my heart and imagination wide open, **my senses discovered infinite wonder and magic inside of everything!**"*
  Then Isaiah explains what hearing God's voice really meant: whenever you see someone being treated unfairly and feel a strong, brave fire inside your heart that says, *"Stop! That is unfair!"*—that brave voice of fairness and truth (**"the voice of honest indignation"**) **is** God's voice speaking inside you! Because Isaiah knew that voice was true, he didn't worry about getting in trouble; he just wrote the truth down bravely.

### Sentence 3

> **Original Text:**
> "Then I asked: “Does a firm persuasion that a thing is so, make it so?”"

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* I then asked him: "Does a deep, unshakable conviction ('a firm persuasion') that something is true actually make it true in reality?"
  *Implied Meaning:* Adopting the skeptical voice of an eighteenth-century Enlightenment philosopher (such as John Locke or David Hume), the narrator tests Isaiah’s claim. To an empirical materialist, truth is merely passive correspondence to external physical facts, so a person's inner conviction cannot alter reality. Blake sets up this question so Isaiah can reveal the creative power of the human imagination.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Blake asks Isaiah a really smart follow-up question: *"Wait a minute—if you believe in something with all your heart ('a firm persuasion'), does believing it so strongly actually make it come true?"*

### Sentence 4

> **Original Text:**
> "He replied: “All poets believe that it does, and in ages of imagination this firm persuasion removed mountains; but many are not capable of a firm persuasion of anything.”"

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* Isaiah answered: "Every true poet knows that it does, and in eras when humanity lived by creative vision ('in ages of imagination'), such unshakable conviction was able to move mountains; however, in our current skeptical age, most people are incapable of holding a deep, wholehearted conviction about anything at all."
  *Implied Meaning:* Alluding to Christ’s teaching in the Gospels that faith can move mountains (Matthew 17:20), Isaiah equates spiritual faith with poetic imagination. In human consciousness, culture, and art, deep conviction shapes reality itself (recall Proverb 33: *"What is now proved was once only imagined"*). Why don't we see "mountains removed" in modern times? Because rationalistic skepticism and timid conformity have made modern minds half-hearted and lukewarm—incapable of committing their whole soul to any vision.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Isaiah smiles and says: *"Every true poet and creator knows that it does!"* In times when people used their big imaginations ("in ages of imagination"), believing in a great idea with all their heart was strong enough to **move mountains** and change the whole world! The only problem, Isaiah says, is that too many people let doubt and fear make them timid, so they never believe in anything strongly enough to try.

### Sentence 5

> **Original Text:**
> "Then Ezekiel said: “The philosophy of the East taught the first principles of human perception; some nations held one principle for the origin, and some another."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* Then Ezekiel spoke up: "The ancient wisdom traditions of the Near and Far East explored the foundational principles of human consciousness and perception; some cultures identified one faculty as the primary origin of all things, while other cultures chose another."
  *Implied Meaning:* Now Ezekiel provides a sweeping philosophical history of ancient thought. Before classical Greek rationalism or modern European empiricism, the ancient cultures of the East recognized that to understand the universe, one must first understand **human perception** (how consciousness experiences and shapes reality). Different civilizations emphasized different faculties (such as cosmic order, natural elements, or mathematical harmony) as the starting point.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Now the second dinner guest, **Ezekiel**, joins the conversation! He explains that thousands of years ago in the ancient East, wise thinkers studied how human minds see, think, and understand the world ("human perception"). Different countries had different ideas about where our thoughts and ideas first come from.

### Sentence 6

> **Original Text:**
> "We of Israel taught that the Poetic Genius (as you now call it) was the first principle, and all the others merely derivative, which was the cause of our despising the Priests and Philosophers of other countries, and prophesying that all Gods would at last be proved to originate in ours, and to be the tributaries of the Poetic Genius."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* "We prophets of ancient Israel taught that the Creative Imagination—which you today call the 'Poetic Genius'—was the supreme, original first principle, and that all other mental faculties, laws, and sciences were merely secondary offshoots ('derivative'); this insight was the true reason we rejected the dogmatic Priests and rationalist Philosophers of neighboring empires, and prophesied that every deity in the world would eventually be shown to have originated from our first principle, flowing like small streams ('tributaries') from the great river of the Poetic Genius."
  *Implied Meaning:* What distinguished ancient prophetic Israel from the priestly and philosophical systems of surrounding empires (such as Egypt, Assyria, or Babylon)? According to Blake’s Ezekiel, Israel’s original insight was recognizing that **the Poetic Genius** (the universal creative Imagination) is the primary source of all human perception and spiritual life, while laws, rituals, and philosophies are merely secondary byproducts ("merely derivative"). When the Hebrew prophets declared that their God was the true origin of all gods, they originally meant that the **Creative Imagination** inside humanity is the true source of every deity ever conceived (echoing Section 5: *"all deities reside in the human breast"*).

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Ezekiel explains that his people taught a wonderful secret: the **Poetic Genius** (which is Blake’s name for the **Spark of Creative Imagination**) is the number-one power inside every human being, and every other skill grows out of it! That is why the prophets didn't listen to the bossy rule-makers ("Priests and Philosophers") in other countries, and why they promised that one day everyone would see that all wisdom flows like little streams ("tributaries") from the giant river of the **Poetic Genius**.

### Sentence 7

> **Original Text:**
> "It was this that our great poet King David desired so fervently, and invokes so pathetically, saying by this he conquers enemies and governs kingdoms; and we so loved our God that we cursed in His name all the deities of surrounding nations, and asserted that they had rebelled."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* "It was this very Poetic Genius that our great poet-king David longed for so passionately and called upon with such deep emotion ('so pathetically') in the Psalms, declaring that through creative inspiration he overcame his foes and guided nations; and we prophets loved this divine Creative Spirit so fiercely that in His name we denounced all the false, authoritarian idols of neighboring empires, declaring that those nations had rebelled against the true creative spirit."
  *Implied Meaning:* Ezekiel reinterprets biblical history through the lens of imagination. King David—traditionally the author of the Book of Psalms—is celebrated here first and foremost as **"our great poet."** When David sang in the Psalms of conquering enemies, he was invoking the power of poetic inspiration and spiritual truth over mental tyranny ("governing kingdoms"). Similarly, when the prophets denounced the gods of Babylon or Tyre, they were attacking dead state idols that had "rebelled" against the living Poetic Genius.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Ezekiel explains that **King David**—who wrote beautiful music and poems called the Psalms on his harp—was a great poet who loved this Creative Spark with all his heart ("invokes so pathetically," which in Blake's time meant with deep, heartfelt feeling). David sang about how creative truth is stronger than any army! And because the prophets loved this creative spirit so much, they spoke out fiercely against the lifeless statues and unfair rules of neighboring kingdoms.

### Sentence 8

> **Original Text:**
> "From these opinions the vulgar came to think that all nations would at last be subject to the Jews."

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* "Because they misunderstood these poetic declarations, literal-minded, uncritical people ('the vulgar') jumped to the conclusion that every nation on earth would eventually be politically conquered and ruled by the Jewish nation."
  *Implied Meaning:* Here Ezekiel directly answers the narrator’s opening question in Sentence 1 about how prophetic poetry gets misunderstood and turned into "imposition"! The prophets were proclaiming a *universal spiritual truth*—that the Poetic Genius should reign supreme in every human heart. However, literal-minded followers ("the vulgar," meaning those who read mechanically rather than imaginatively) mistook a metaphor of spiritual liberation for narrow political nationalism and empire-building.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Sadly, people who didn't understand poetry ("the vulgar") got confused by the prophets' words: instead of realizing that Ezekiel meant **Imagination and Truth** should win in everyone's heart around the world, they thought he meant their own country was going to become the boss over every other country!

### Sentence 9

> **Original Text:**
> "“This,” said he, “like all firm persuasions, is come to pass, for all nations believe the Jews’ code, and worship the Jews’ God; and what greater subjection can be?”"

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* "'And this belief,' Ezekiel continued with dark irony, 'just like every other deeply held conviction ("firm persuasion"), has actually come true—for today the nations of Christendom have adopted the ancient legalistic rulebook ("the Jews’ code") and worship a literalized, authoritarian Lawgiver; and what greater mental subjugation could possibly exist?'"
  *Implied Meaning:* Ezekiel points out a devastating historical irony. Earlier, in Sentence 4, Isaiah said that a "firm persuasion" makes things come to pass. Because literal-minded priests and followers across history firmly believed in a narrow, legalistic code of domination rather than liberating Poetic Genius, *that* misinterpretation came true! Throughout Europe, nations came to worship an external, punitive Lawgiver and obey a rigid moral rulebook ("the Ten Commandments")—resulting in the mental and spiritual "subjection" of the world to priestly law rather than poetic freedom.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**
  Ezekiel sighs and points out something ironic: remember how Isaiah said that believing something strongly ("a firm persuasion") can make it happen? Because so many people believed in strict **rulebooks ("code")** instead of free **imagination**, the strict rulebooks ended up taking over the world! Ezekiel asks: *Isn't having your mind locked up by strict rulebooks the biggest trap ("subjection") of all?*

Isaiah’s reply states a profound spiritual law: authentic imaginative vision is eternal and indestructible. Just as Proverb 10 declared that *"Eternity is in love with the productions of time,"* no truly essential work of the Poetic Genius ever perishes from universal consciousness.

### Sentence 14

> **Original Text:**

* **Sentence Translation and Detailed Implied Meaning (For Adults):**
  *Translation:* Isaiah replied: "The exact same spirit and purpose that motivated our Greek friend, the philosopher Diogenes."
  *Implied Meaning:* By comparing himself to **"our friend Diogenes the Grecian"**—the famous Greek Cynic philosopher who lived in a simple wooden tub, rejected worldly luxury and social pretension, and carried a lantern in daylight searching for an honest man—Isaiah reveals the universal brotherhood of radical truth-tellers. Both Hebrew prophets and Greek philosophers used bold, unconventional living to strip away society’s false pride, materialism, and hypocrisy, demonstrating that humans need no artificial status symbols to live in truth.

* **Detailed Explanation (For an 8-Year-Old):**

Why would a wise person do something so hard and uncomfortable?

### Sentence 18

## Comprehensive Summaries of Section 6: "A Memorable Fancy" (The Prophets at Dinner)

### 1. Detailed Summary for Adults and Educators

In **Section 6 (Plates 12–13)**, William Blake uses an imaginative dinner conversation with the Old Testament prophets **Isaiah** and **Ezekiel** to redefine prophecy, inspiration, and artistic integrity:

* **Prophecy as Expanded Perception and Honest Indignation:** Isaiah clarifies that prophetic inspiration is not the literal hearing of an external voice with the physical ear ("finite organical perception"), but the expansion of the senses to **discover the infinite in everything**. Furthermore, the true voice of God within humanity is **"the voice of honest indignation"**—the righteous, passionate refusal to tolerate injustice and falsehood.
* **The Poetic Genius and Its Literalization:** Ezekiel explains that ancient Israel originally recognized the **Poetic Genius** (the universal creative Imagination) as the first principle of human perception, celebrated by poets like King David. However, when the prophets poetically denounced foreign state idols in the name of the Poetic Genius, literal-minded followers ("the vulgar") misinterpreted their words as a promise of nationalistic and legalistic domination—ironically resulting in the world's subjection to rigid dogmatic codes rather than imaginative freedom.
* **Visionary Action and Universal Kinship:** Both prophets affirm that no truly essential work of imagination is ever lost, and they defend their radical symbolic actions—comparing them to the Greek philosopher Diogenes and the spiritual practices of Indigenous North American tribes—as deliberate efforts to shock humanity awake into "a perception of the infinite." The section concludes with a timeless ethical challenge: never betray your inner genius or conscience for the sake of conventional ease.

---

### 2. Detailed Summary for an 8-Year-Old Learner

In **Section 6: A Memorable Fancy**, William Blake invites two famous wise writers from the Bible—**Isaiah** and **Ezekiel**—over for dinner to learn their secrets:

1. **How to Hear the Voice of Truth:** Isaiah explains that he didn't see God with his regular eyes or ears; instead, he opened his heart to see **infinite wonder inside everything**. Whenever you feel a brave voice inside your heart standing up for fairness and truth (**"honest indignation"**), that is the voice of goodness speaking through you!
2. **Believing Can Move Mountains:** Isaiah and Ezekiel explain that the **Poetic Genius** (our Creative Imagination) is the greatest power inside us. When we believe in a true, creative idea with all our heart ("a firm persuasion"), that belief is strong enough to move mountains—though we have to be careful never to turn living poems into bossy rulebooks!
3. **Never Hide Your Special Gift:** At the end of dinner, Isaiah and Ezekiel explain why they did such bold, unusual things in their lives (just like the Greek thinker Diogenes and Native American wise seekers): they wanted to act like an alarm clock to wake people up to the wonder of the universe! Most of all, they remind us never to hide our conscience or our creative gifts just to take the easy way out.
